Variance Reduction for Decentralized Stochastic Optimization with Multiple Local Updates

具有多次局部更新的去中心化随机优化的方差减少方法

Multiple local updates are widely used in decentralized optimization to enhance communication efficiency, often in conjunction with stochastic gradients when exact gradient computation is costly. However, such algorithms generally converge only to a neighborhood of the optimum, whose size depends on the stepsize and the variance of gradient noise. Variance reduction has been incorporated into ADMM-based methods to achieve exact convergence in expectation, but their stepsizes typically scale inversely with both the square root of the network size and the cube of the number of local updates, leading to slow convergence as the network size or the number of local updates increases. In this paper, we propose a novel primal-dual-based decentralized optimization algorithm that integrates variance reduction with multiple local updates, with a stepsize independent of the network size and inversely proportional to the number of local updates (rather than its cube), thereby enabling significantly larger stepsizes and faster convergence. Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, we establish the first linear convergence guarantee in expectation for decentralized optimization with multiple local updates under stochastic gradients in the strongly convex setting. Beyond the theoretical guarantee, the proposed method exchanges only one variable per round, halving the communication cost compared with gradient-tracking-based methods. Numerical experiments further demonstrate its advantages in convergence speed and accuracy.

Regret-Optimal Wasserstein-Robust Linear Regression

后悔最优Wasserstein鲁棒线性回归

Distributionally robust optimization (DRO) minimizes worst-case risk under distributional uncertainty, but its decisions can be suboptimal in terms of their excess risk, or regret. We study this gap for multivariate least-squares regression, assuming that the observed source law and unknown target law differ by an adversarial 2-Wasserstein perturbation of size $\varepsilon$. We first identify the asymptotic complexity of robust decision-making in the $\varepsilon \to 0$ limit, characterized by a problem-specific condition number $κ$. Here, a wide spectrum of simple algorithms, including DRO, achieve the instance-optimal rate of $O(κ\varepsilon^2)$. For fixed $\varepsilon > 0$, we prove that DRO still achieves the minimax rate if the problem has bounded residual effective rank. On the other hand, we identify significant failure modes where DRO is provably suboptimal by dimension-dependent factors. Fortunately, we prove that two alternative procedures, minimax regret optimization (MRO) and spectral-norm regularization (SNR), attain the minimax rate. We derive an exact convex reformulation of MRO and an accelerated proximal algorithm for SNR, along with high-probability finite-sample guarantees. We support these theoretical results with numerical experiments separating the performance of MRO and SNR from DRO.

Here, we introduce Chronos-2, a foundation model for zero-shot forecasting that models the interactions between multivariate time series and heterogeneous covariates, which commonly arise in scientific applications. In addition, we demonstrate its effectiveness in diverse scientific settings -- including infectious-disease forecasting, macroeconomic modeling, carbon-flux prediction, and runoff simulation -- where the model matches or surpasses specialized methods developed for each domain. These results show that a single pretrained model can capture structure shared across temporal systems.

The rapid growth of model complexity in emerging applications has significantly amplified the challenges of achieving data-efficient RL, particularly in sample-starved scenarios where data collection may be costly, time-consuming, or even high-stakes. Consequently, understanding and improving the data efficiency of RL algorithms has become a central problem of both fundamental theoretical interest and practical importance. Despite decades of research, the statistical underpinnings of RL remained remarkably underdeveloped until relatively recently, particularly in the finite-sample regimes most relevant to modern practice. Over the past few years, there has been a surge of activity advancing the frontiers of non-asymptotic statistical RL theory, drawing on ideas from high-dimensional statistics and probability, information theory, and statistical learning theory. Centered around non-asymptotic sample complexity analysis, our treatment emphasizes minimax-optimal algorithm design, particularly methods that attain statistical optimality without significant burn-in costs, as well as sharp statistical characterizations of several widely used RL algorithms. Whenever possible, we provide largely self-contained analyses together with intuitive explanations. We also briefly discuss several extensions, such as robust RL, competitive multi-agent RL, federated RL, uncertainty quantification, and RL from human feedback.

Anomalies as Residuals: Cauchy Displacement Networks

Neighbourhood-based detectors such as KNN, LOF and LUNAR remain among the most accurate methods for tabular anomaly detection, but they compare every query with the stored training set, so their inference cost and memory grow with the data and the data must be kept at deployment. We ask whether the neighbourhood geometry these detectors exploit can be compiled into a small network that scores a query with a single forward pass. We propose the Cauchy Displacement Network (CDN), which regresses each normal point onto the mean of its $k$ nearest training neighbours with a single-hidden-layer network of Cauchy activations and scores a query by the length of its predicted displacement. This displacement is an empirical mean-shift vector, whose fixed-radius population counterpart locally approximates a scaled gradient of the log density. We show that Cauchy units exactly represent the score fields of a structured family of Student-$t$ expert densities, and establish a constructive approximation bound for the corresponding population neighbourhood-mean map using a single-hidden-layer Cauchy network. We further show that the bounded network output guarantees asymptotically linear residual-score growth along every fixed direction. On 46 benchmark datasets against 28 baselines, CDN obtains the best mean rank under both AUC-ROC and AUC-PR, while scoring $10^4$ queries in a few milliseconds on a single CPU thread, up to three orders of magnitude faster than neighbourhood-based detectors, with a model whose size does not depend on the training set.

核心方法： Proposes Cauchy Displacement Network (CDN) for anomaly detection, which replaces traditional neighborhood-based methods (like KNN, LOF) with a small neural network using Cauchy activations to predict displacement vectors. Differential rank preservation manifold learning

差分排名保持流形学习

Manifold learning methods like t-SNE and UMAP preserve local neighborhoods, but they do not preserve the ranking of the neighbors inside them. Two embeddings can return the same neighbor set while disagreeing on which point is closest. This loss of order degrades downstream applications that value the order in the embedding. We propose Smooth Rank t-SNE (SR-tSNE), which adds a differentiable rank term to the standard t-SNE loss. To enable efficient training, this term uses a sigmoid rank approximation to explicitly force a point's true high-dimensional neighbors to outrank unrelated intruders and retain their original order. We also introduce Ordinal DLP (O-DLP), an extension of the Degree of Locality Preservation (DLP) metric that credits a neighbor only when it returns at its original rank. Across eight benchmarks spanning image, text, speech, remote-sensing and single-cell data, SR-tSNE outperforms 12 baselines in rank preservation and neighborhood overlap. Ablation studies demonstrate our rank term alone boosts O-DLP by 5% to 28%, a gain not achievable using standard pairwise loss. This advantage transfers to downstream applications. SR-tSNE achieves the best label-transfer performance across seven vision datasets. For cell-type discovery across six single-cell datasets, it ranks first in 17 of 18 dataset-metric combinations, whether clustering resolution is selected with or without cell-type labels. In intraday trading, SR-tSNE achieves higher returns, lower risk, and lower turnover than the baselines. To our knowledge, SR-tSNE is the first t-SNE-family method with explicit order preservation.

核心方法： Smooth Rank t-SNE (SR-tSNE) enhances traditional t-SNE by introducing a differentiable rank term in the loss function to explicitly preserve the order of nearest neighbors.
