Auto-Alpha: A Framework for Autonomous Factor Research

Auto-Alpha：自主因子研究框架

Autonomous factor research requires generating economic hypotheses as well as implementing and testing them. We present Auto-Alpha, a framework that connects report-guided initialization to self-directed research through a test-driven implementation loop. An idea agent proposes economic mechanisms and signal constructions; a research agent develops them with backtest feedback. Semantic audit, quantitative review, and human admission select factors for a persistent library, while an Experience Library retains research insights. Starting from 120 research reports, Auto-Alpha builds an initial library of 32 factors. It then generates new ideas and refines existing candidates, producing 420 implementations and retaining 62 additional factors. We evaluate factor quality and portfolio performance using a fixed library snapshot of 105 factors. The median 1-day Rank IC is 0.0301, and a weekly Top-300 portfolio returns 16.81% annualized after stated costs over 2020-2026, versus 1.71% for the CSI 1000 price index. These retrospective results document an operating research process and its artifacts; controlled evaluation is needed to isolate the contribution of its components.

Learning with Local Search MCMC Layers

基于局部搜索MCMC层的学习方法

Integrating combinatorial optimization layers into neural networks has recently attracted significant research interest. However, many existing approaches lack theoretical guarantees or fail to perform adequately when relying on inexact solvers. This is a critical limitation, as many operations research problems are NP-hard, often necessitating the use of neighborhood-based local search heuristics. In this paper, we introduce a principled approach for learning with such inexact solvers. Inspired by the connection between simulated annealing and Metropolis-Hastings, we transform the problem specific neighborhood systems used in local search heuristics into proposal distributions, implementing MCMC on the set of feasible solutions. This allows us to construct differentiable, stochastic combinatorial layers and associated loss functions. Replacing an exact solver with a local search strongly reduces the computational burden of learning on many applications. We demonstrate our approach on a dynamic vehicle routing problem with time windows, binary vector and k-subset prediction tasks, as well as a multi-dimensional knapsack decision-focused learning problem.

Falsifiable Compositional Reasoning in Time Series Forecasting

时间序列预测中的可证伪组合推理

Time series foundation models as well as the language-guided analytical agents achieve strong accuracy on standard chronological benchmarks. However, models that rank highest on historical validation splits often degrade across non-stationary regime transitions. We hypothesise that this gap arises from using historical backtesting as an open-ended search and model-selection objective instead of using it to test pre-specified compositional assumptions. Real-world time series are generated by recurring latent primitives that co-move within finite historical windows. Under strong historical co-movement, chronological holdout error cannot separate predictors that learn invariant couplings among primitives from predictors that fit transient historical correlations. Searching over large pools of architectures, hyperparameters, or chain-of-thought rules further biases selection toward historical shortcuts. Through theoretical analysis and diagnostic experiments on a controlled multi-primitive benchmark and German day-ahead electricity prices, we show that expanding validation search fails to improve out-of-regime accuracy, and that decomposing primitives without an ex-ante falsification test still breaks down when residual correlations shift. Hence, we propose formulating time series forecasting and reasoning around falsifiable compositional hypotheses that combine neural primitive encoders, explicit coupling constraints, and training-calibrated falsification tests.

A new algorithmic approach for finding flat minima: critically damped Polyak stepsizes

寻找平坦最小值的新算法方法：临界阻尼Polyak步长

We present a new adaptive stepsize rule that biases gradient descent toward flatter minima in both theory and practice, and establish the first uniform-in-time guarantees for both sharpness and loss reduction for a broad class of locally quadratic objectives. Our critically damped Polyak (CDP) stepsizes multiply the classical Polyak rule by $4$ and a mild damping factor. The factor $4$ is critical in that it produces equioscillations around the manifold of minimizers $\mathcal{M}$ to leading order. Damping provably drives convergence to $\mathcal{M}$. CDP thus operates at the edge of stability by adapting its stepsizes to local curvature. Once the loss is small, its projected iterates follow gradient descent on $\log{\lambda_1(\nabla^2 \mathcal{L})}$ along $\mathcal{M}$ to leading order, paralleling the flatness bias of normalized gradient descent (Arora et al., 2022). Unlike normalized gradient descent, CDP step lengths shrink with the distance to $\mathcal{M}$, enabling convergence to an interpolating solution that locally minimizes sharpness at finite learning rate. We establish our theory for a broad class of non-negative losses with locally quadratic Morse-Bott structure near $\mathcal{M}$, capturing regular interpolation regimes in smooth overparameterized neural networks. Empirically, CDP stepsizes produce a flattening effect similar to that of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM, Foret et al., 2021), reducing sharpness even in settings beyond our theory, including nonlinear teacher-student networks and fine-tuning of pre-trained LLMs.

Standard optimizers such as Adam use fixed gradient-based statistics, while learned PINN optimizers replace the fixed rule with a learned update built from local parameters and optimization-history features. However, none explicitly use PDE-level information. We study two coupled design questions: what PDE-level information should a learned optimizer observe, and how should that information influence its update? We introduce an 11-dimensional PDE state-capturing operator structure, residual geometry, and short-horizon update response, and use it to condition a structured meta-learned optimizer through bounded feature-wise modulation. Repeated design studies identify the compact residual-geometry state G2 and bounded FiLM as the strongest overall choices among those tested. The resulting PDE-State-conditioned optimizer improves all evaluation metrics (relative-L2, L1, solution MSE, and physics MSE) over Adam and the unconditioned learned optimizer on Linear Advection, KdV, and Burgers.

Scone: Symmetry-aware Neural Combinatorial Optimization for Graph Coloring

Graph coloring, which entails assigning colors to nodes such that no adjacent nodes have the same color, is a fundamental combinatorial optimization problem. In general, however, finding a node coloring with no monochromatic edges given a fixed palette is NP-hard. This motivates learning-based methods that can learn to exploit recurring structure across instances to efficiently produce high-quality colorings. Crucially, the validity of a coloring is invariant to permutations of the colors, and we show that exploiting this symmetry reduces the size of the effective state space over which the model has to learn its policy. At the same time, the coloring algorithm must be able to break some symmetries, for example to assign different colors to two adjacent nodes belonging to the same automorphism orbit. In this paper, we introduce Scone, a model that uses a color-equivariant graph neural network to construct colorings sequentially, allowing information from previous assignments to propagate and break node symmetries. Scone achieves perfect solution quality with low inference times across several graph coloring tasks, including on sudoku_extreme and register allocations, and showcases generalization to graph families not encountered during training.

Multiple Criteria Decision Making Under XORness

How should we make decisions under XORness? XORness is a class of uncertainty arising from indeterminacy, exclusivity, and hesitancy. We develop a multiple criteria decision making framework that preserves XORness throughout the decision process. We define XOR numbers and prove their fundamental arithmetic and algebraic properties. Building on these results, we extend the deterministic decision matrix to an XOR decision matrix and introduce a compatible normalization procedure. Further, we propose an additive XOR aggregation operator, which preserves indeterminacy, exclusivity, and hesitancy inherent in evaluations. In addition, we introduce an axiomatic XORness measure that quantifies the degree of uncertainty associated with global performance scores and develop a performance--XORness index to rank alternatives. We prove that this index induces a total preorder over the set of alternatives and identify the conditions for preserving dominance. The applicability of the developed framework is demonstrated through the risk assessment of cyberthreat scenarios in energy systems, and recommendations are derived using the XOR--weighted sum method.

SenseAlign: Learning Decision-Aligned Predictive Representations for Active Urban Sensing with UAVs

Active urban sensing orchestrates a mobile sensor fleet to proactively inspect a dynamic road network and gather critical information for system management. This problem simultaneously requires understanding the complex spatio-temporal evolution and making decisions with partial observations. In this work, we focus on active sensing in a large-scale urban road network with utilities grounded in dynamic traffic states, while prior work has rarely considered these factors jointly. We propose \emph{SenseAlign}, a Reinforcement Learning-based (RL) framework that learns decision-aligned predictive representations for coordinating an urban sensing drone fleet. SenseAlign first trains a privileged teacher agent that exploits future ground-truth traffic for optimal sensing choices. The student agent has an auxiliary branch that learns to predict future traffic with partial observations, in addition to the RL-trained actor and critic branches. During student learning, our framework matches the action distributions of the teacher under ground-truth and student-predicted future traffic, while keeping the teacher's observations unchanged. Such a design shapes the latent space for decision-making instead of prediction alone. At inference time, the student agent has no overhead as all this learning is done in training. We conduct comprehensive experiments with the real road network of central Barcelona containing 1570 road segments, and demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms the baselines on multiple utility formulations. The codebase will be made publicly available upon paper acceptance.

Bayesian optimization on amortized search space

In real-world black-box optimization problems, a mechanistic simulator is often available that approximately predicts the behavior of the system as a function of the parameters being optimized. Here, we show how an amortized inverse map of such a simulator can be used in Bayesian optimization to restrict the search space while using a Gaussian process kernel that is aware of the original problem geometry. This allows for efficiently leveraging the low intrinsic dimensionality of the optimization problem. We characterize its approximation bias and information gain, and derive an intrinsic-dimension-dependent regret rate under additional geometric and Gaussian Process assumptions. We illustrate the method with an application to drug dosing personalization in Parkinson's disease. We use, as a simulator, a pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) model of a drug, a mechanistic model calibrated on population data with quantified parameter uncertainty. A dosing neural network is trained offline against the model, amortizing the map from patient PK/PD parameters to the regimen predicted to be optimal, and online Bayesian optimization then searches over this learned, low-dimensional representation rather than over raw dosages. Numerical simulations demonstrate the improved performance of our method in this application, as well as under simulator misspecification.

Cross-Behavior Policy Optimization: Unified Learning from Offline Demonstrations and On- and Off-Policy Rollouts via a Shared Regression Target

Reasoning post-training draws on offline demonstrations and policy-generated rollouts, yet conventional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) followed by reinforcement learning (RL) separates their objectives and limits continued reuse of collected responses. We propose \textbf{Cross-Behavior Policy Optimization} (CBPO), which makes these sources compatible with one regression loss through a fixed-reference target. Because the generating policy does not enter this target, positive and negative offline completions, reference responses, and current and historical rollouts can share a common reward-labeled record format and replay buffer. As new responses enter the buffer, optimization retains the same regression objective without coordinating separate SFT and RL objectives or requiring stored behavior-policy probabilities, importance weighting, or a learned critic. Restricting the available sources gives offline, on-policy, and off-policy variants, while CBPO-Unified combines them. Across two model scales and four mathematical reasoning benchmarks, CBPO delivers leading \texttt{avg@32} and \texttt{pass@32} performance across benchmarks and data regimes. On Qwen3-1.7B, CBPO-Unified improves benchmark-averaged \texttt{avg@32} and \texttt{pass@32} over SFT DAPO by 2.33 and 4.44 percentage points, respectively. In the replay ablation, retaining historical rollouts improves these metrics by 1.87 and 3.72 percentage points over the no-history configuration.
