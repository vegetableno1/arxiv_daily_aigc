NoLBERT-Calls: Reading Earnings Calls Without Hindsight

NoLBERT-Calls：无后见之明解读财报电话会议

Language models are now a standard tool for measuring what managers say on earnings calls, but the encoders behind these measures were usually pretrained on text from the same years a backtest covers. Holding out a test period does not remove that hindsight, because it sits in the features. We adapt NoLBERT, an encoder pretrained only on text published between 1976 and 1995, to earnings calls without letting it read anything dated after 31 December 2023. The calls supply the training signal themselves: every analyst question is followed by management's answer, and the model learns to pick that answer out from the other answers of the same call. We test on every US call from January 2025 to October 2026 (20,323 calls, 254,575 questions) and make every modeling choice on calls from 2024. The adapted model, NoLBERT-Calls-2023, ranks the true answer first for 78.0% of questions (95% interval 77.8-78.2; chance 8.0%), against 26.3% before adaptation. It is 1.0 point ahead of a call-adapted ChronoBERT, 3.4 points ahead of a call-adapted FinBERT, and 18.7 and 19.8 points ahead of the best off-the-shelf retriever and BM25. A year-by-year test over 2013-2026 finds no look-ahead premium: relative to a model adapted only through 2012, the released model does no better on the years it was trained on than on years it never saw. The price of the cutoff is compute: under one shared 150-minute recipe NoLBERT adapts least well of four encoders, and its lead comes from a longer, staged adaptation of about 13 GPU-hours.

核心方法： The paper introduces NoLBERT-Calls, a language model adapted for earnings call analysis without leveraging future information (hindsight). The model is pretrained on historical text (1976-1995) and fine-tuned on earnings calls up to 2023, using a novel training signal where the model learns to identify management's answers to analyst questions. The approach avoids data leakage by strictly partitioning training and test periods.
数据来源： US earnings calls from January 2025 to October 2026 (20,323 calls with 254,575 questions), with validation on 2024 data. Historical text from 1976-1995 for pretraining.
Alpha 潜力：

The model demonstrates strong performance (78% accuracy in identifying correct answers) and outperforms other adapted models (e.g., ChronoBERT, FinBERT) and off-the-shelf retrievers. This suggests it could generate alpha by extracting nuanced signals from earnings calls more reliably, without the bias introduced by future information. The lack of a 'look-ahead premium' indicates robustness for real-time applications.

AllocFlow: End-to-End Portfolio Trading Action Generation from Raw Market Data

AllocFlow：从原始市场数据到投资组合交易操作的端到端生成

Real-world portfolio management requires converting noisy market observations into coordinated, simultaneous allocations across multiple assets under shared capital constraints. Advances in forecasting models and alpha discovery help identify opportunities. However, traditional pipelines often decouple signal forecasting from portfolio construction, potentially misaligning learning with allocation and execution objectives. We present AllocFlow, an end-to-end flow-matching framework that directly generates joint portfolio allocations from raw multi-asset market histories. To capture dependencies among portfolio positions, AllocFlow employs a cross-asset flow Transformer conditioned on temporal market representations to generate continuous, signed position weights across all instruments jointly. To address the sample inefficiency and instability of learning continuous allocation policies from scratch, we establish a two-stage training scheme: strategy-supervised imitation shapes a structured distribution of candidate portfolios, followed by group-relative reinforcement learning (RL) that refines joint decisions using portfolio-level financial rewards and a frozen policy reference. On hourly cryptocurrency perpetual futures, RL refinement raises the annualized net return of the imitation policy from $27.79\%$ to $51.38\%$ and its Calmar ratio from $0.955$ to $4.043$, surpassing the teacher strategy on every metric, and AllocFlow reaches a Calmar ratio of $4.90$ on six further months of unseen 2026 data without retraining.

核心方法： AllocFlow is an end-to-end flow-matching framework that uses a cross-asset flow Transformer to generate joint portfolio allocations directly from raw multi-asset market histories. It employs a two-stage training scheme: strategy-supervised imitation learning followed by group-relative reinforcement learning (RL) to refine joint decisions using portfolio-level financial rewards.

条件定价Transformer模型

In the first stage, a transformer estimates conditional expected returns, and kernel-weighted principal component analysis of its residuals estimates the local factor loading space. Projecting the conditional mean onto this space separates factor risk compensation from conditional mispricing. Finally, simulations and empirical studies demonstrate the predictive and interpretive benefits of the proposed method.

PIRanNN: 跳跃扩散和随机波动模型下的随机特征定价和校准

We study a random-feature method for pricing and calibrating European options under jump-diffusion (Merton, Kou) and stochastic-volatility (Heston, SABR) models. The network has one hidden layer with a probit activation and fixed random inner weights, so fitting it to the pricing equation, the payoff and the boundary conditions is a single regularised least-squares problem, with no iterative training. For Merton and Kou the jump integral of each feature is explicit, so no quadrature is needed. One factorisation of the least-squares matrix gives the price at every state and time, its Greeks, and the exact derivatives of the prices with respect to the model parameters for gradient-based calibration. For the jump models, subtracting the Black-Scholes price removes the payoff kink and reduces the pricing error by about two orders of magnitude. The method is then about as accurate as a standard Crank-Nicolson finite-difference scheme at the same run time, although a finite-difference scheme that also subtracts the Black-Scholes price is about two orders of magnitude more accurate still. On the test problems of a recent physics-informed neural network study, the method matches that network's accuracy in seconds instead of about half an hour. The calibration experiments show that a solver can fit the quotes and still return wrong parameters when its pricing error is not small compared with the effect of the parameters on the prices: without the subtraction the solver misses the jump parameters by up to 50%, and with it they are recovered to within 3%. For Heston and SABR, used as an applicability study with boundary conditions that need no reference prices, pricing errors are about one percent and the calibrated parameters are within about 15% of the true values.

Risk Contribution (RC) is a widely used risk attribution metric that assigns each position a share of portfolio volatility, with contributions that sum to total portfolio volatility. This paper proposes a leave-one-out decomposition of RC into two interpretable components at the position level: an inherent term (position's own scaled variance) and a correlation term (covariance with the other holdings). Unlike the X-sigma-rho framework, which uses the position's correlation with the full portfolio, ICD uses covariance with the other holdings, yielding qualitatively different diagnostic signals when a position is large relative to the portfolio. The contribution is the interpretive framing and an exact identity linking the components to incremental volatility (iVol), which allows the discrete condition under which a position reduces portfolio volatility to be stated in closed form. That condition differs from the marginal condition and the two can disagree for over-hedged or levered positions. The decomposition also inherits RC's strict additivity. Empirical illustrations on three multi-asset portfolios show the components shift differently across regimes, distinguishing stand-alone volatility spikes from correlation breakdown, and demonstrate value for risk reporting, stress testing, and attribution.
