EVOLVE: Uncertainty-Aware Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Prediction with Adaptive Prototype Memories

EVOLVE：基于自适应原型记忆的不确定性感知多元时间序列异常预测

Unsupervised multivariate time series anomaly prediction (AP) aims to estimate future anomaly risks from current observations, providing early-warning signals for proactive intervention and maintenance in complex systems. Existing unsupervised AP methods follow a perturbation-based paradigm, where predefined or random perturbations are injected into historical windows to simulate abnormal future evolution. However, these methods share perturbations across different variables and contexts, and learn deterministic history-to-future evolution. In reality, anomaly evolution is variable- and context-dependent: the same perturbation may be amplified into abnormal future dynamics in one variable or context, while being absorbed as normal fluctuation in another. In addition, future evolution is not uniquely determined by a historical window, since similar histories may lead to multiple plausible trajectories, such as gradual degradation or abrupt changes. To address these problems, we propose EVOLVE, a unified framework that models unsupervised AP via variable- and context-conditioned perturbations and uncertainty-aware future reasoning. EVOLVE constructs context-specific anomaly perturbations through an adaptive anomaly prototype memory, which can dynamically update and retrieve relevant perturbations at the variable level. EVOLVE models uncertainty by sampling multiple latent historical states and decoding through complementary smooth and deviation-amplifying evolution hypotheses, and the disagreement among decoded future trajectories is used to estimate future anomaly risk. Extensive experiments on six real-world benchmark datasets demonstrate that EVOLVE consistently outperforms state-of-the-art anomaly prediction baselines.

核心方法： EVOLVE introduces a framework for unsupervised multivariate time series anomaly prediction using adaptive anomaly prototype memories and uncertainty-aware future reasoning. It models future anomaly risks by considering variable- and context-conditioned perturbations, and by sampling multiple latent historical states to decode future trajectories through complementary evolution hypotheses.

Groupwise low-rank representations capture shared structure across experts while retaining expert-specific projections, and Nyström-based neuron compression reduces intermediate width within each expert. DT-JEPA: Learning to Predict Future Representations for Decision-Making

DT-JEPA：通过学习预测未来表示进行决策

Decision Transformer (DT) has demonstrated strong performance in various offline reinforcement learning (RL) tasks by conditioning action generation on expected future returns and historical trajectories. However, treating expected future returns as the condition provides an incomplete description of the future, as trajectories with different state transitions and behaviors may share similar returns. Moreover, expected future returns inherently depend on task-specific reward functions, which makes policy learning reliant on reward-labeled datasets and restricts its scalability to tasks with similar but unseen rewards. Recently, Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) have emerged as a promising paradigm of self-supervised learning-based world models, which learn predictive representations of future dynamics in latent space rather than reconstructing future states in the original space. Inspired by JEPA, we propose DT-JEPA, an offline pretraining and online fine-tuning framework that learns general knowledge from reward-free offline trajectories and achieves excellent adaptation to unseen tasks. Instead of predicting a single deterministic future embedding, we design a novel probabilistic predictor to model the conditional distribution of future representations in the latent space, thereby capturing both predictive information and uncertainty about future dynamics. Theoretically, we show that 1) the JEPA objective seeks to maximize a variational lower bound on the mutual information between historical representations and future trajectories; and 2) predictive future-conditioned policy learning admits a finite-sample performance bound with an O(N-1/4) estimation term. Experiments on D4RL benchmarks demonstrate that DT-JEPA achieves efficient adaptation across varying offline RL tasks, outperforming traditional DT-based and other offline RL methods.

Contextual Decision Making: Predictive or Generative

情境决策：预测还是生成？

Contextual decision making uses observed covariates to guide decisions under uncertainty. Two natural approaches are predict-then-optimize (PtO), which learns the conditional expected objective, and generate-then-optimize (GtO), which learns conditional uncertainty and optimizes using generated scenarios. We study when each approach is preferable by examining how the choice of learning target affects statistical accuracy and optimization tractability. Although PtO learns a narrower target, our regret bounds show how differences in smoothness can offset GtO's additional dimensional burden. Exploiting smoothness also raises an optimization tradeoff: within the class of nonnegative-weight PtO methods studied, a lower bound limits the gains from additional smoothness, while more flexible methods may introduce signed weights and lose the guarantee of preserving convexity. GtO preserves scenario-wise convexity, but requires learning a richer object and generating scenarios for optimization. Synthetic experiments and a real case study illustrate how these tradeoffs vary with data availability and problem structure. Together with the scope for reusing a model across tasks, our findings provide practical guidance for choosing between the approaches without identifying a universal winner.

Optimal collective investment under regulatory uncertainty

监管不确定性下的最优集体投资

We study optimal collective investment subject to a Value-at-Risk (VaR)-type constraint that accounts for uncertainty about future regulatory requirements. The constraint incorporates several possible wealth thresholds and imposes a minimum weighted average probability of meeting them. We characterize the optimal terminal wealth, which combines regions of constant wealth at regulatory thresholds with Merton-type investment regions. Unlike a classical VaR constraint with only one threshold, our formulation generates protection against several thresholds. For two possible thresholds, we identify when both are protected and when protection concentrates on the larger one. Numerical results show that the regulatory constraint can substantially reshape the optimal payoff and generate large welfare losses relative to unconstrained investment when the required compliance probability is high, even the smaller wealth threshold is high, or initial capital is limited. In heterogeneous investor pools, welfare losses depend strongly on the composition of the risk aversions and the Pareto weights governing risk sharing. Losses are particularly pronounced when highly risk-averse investors dominate the pool. Moreover, investors with smaller capital contributions can bear disproportionately large shares of the total welfare loss, while larger contributors may bear less than their capital-proportional share. Thus, regulatory uncertainty not only reduces aggregate welfare but can also create substantial disparities in how its costs are distributed among investors.

Learning Positive Linear Combinations of ReLUs in Fully Polynomial Time

在全多项式时间内学习ReLU正线性组合

We study the fundamental problem of PAC learning positive linear combinations of $k$ arbitrary ReLU activations on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with respect to the Gaussian distribution, in $L_2$ loss. We give the first fully-polynomial time algorithm for this learning task. Specifically, our algorithm draws $\mathrm{poly}(d, k, 1/ε)$ samples, runs in $\mathrm{poly}(d, k, 1/ε)$ time, and outputs a hypothesis that is $ε$-close to the target concept with high probability. All prior algorithms for this problem require super-polynomial time in $k$ or $1/ε$, or leverage strong well-conditioned assumptions on the weight matrix. On a technical level, our approach develops a novel boosting procedure of a ``weak'' learner that recovers the hidden weight vectors to constant error, in a list-decodable manner. To develop this ``weak'' learning procedure, we leverage implicit tensor computation techniques to construct a low-degree filtering procedure based on Hermite tensors of the target function. This procedure amplifies separation among (clusters of) weight vectors to learn each of them to constant error. We then argue that the same procedure can be used for improving the estimation of hidden weight vectors, using gradient descent-like updates on the Euclidean sphere. A crucial ingredient of our algorithm is a pruning approach that ensures only relevant vectors that approximate the hidden weight vectors at target accuracy are maintained by the algorithm.

This strategy involves maximizing a target objective under a single sample drawn from the current belief over unknown quantities. Random Jump Forests

Random jump forests are introduced as a nonparametric tool for estimating conditional cumulative transition rates and occupation probabilities in multi-state models. The main idea is that a single tree partitions the covariate space for all transitions simultaneously, using partially observed trajectories, and with Nelson--Aalen estimators at the terminal nodes. Occupation probabilities are recovered through the product integral without requiring the Markov assumption. Uniform consistency and a functional central limit theorem for the estimators of cumulative rates can be established under the assumption that the covariate vector takes finitely many values, and under suitable splitting conditions. In particular, pointwise confidence intervals and simultaneous bands are available in the Markov case. Simulation studies show a predictive accuracy close to the correctly specified benchmark models and marked improvements when benchmarks are misspecified, with further illustration on four biomedical data sets. In the coverage study, confidence bands attain near-nominal coverage in several settings, with pooling of heterogeneous rates being the main source of possible undercoverage. The method, including prediction assessment and variable importance, is implemented in the R package JumpForests.
