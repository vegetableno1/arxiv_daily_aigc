TSFM Meets LLM: Context as Covariates

TSFM 与 LLM 的结合：上下文作为协变量

Textual information is ubiquitous in real-world time series datasets, appearing as news, reports, and annotations that hold key predictive signals not deducible from numerical history alone. Large Language Models (LLMs) interpret text but lack numerical precision, while Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) are quantitatively accurate but cannot reason about the effects of text. Moreover, the lack of large-scale paired multimodal datasets limits progress in bridging this gap. In this work, we address this challenge by providing a scalable approach for generating realistic multimodal datasets by annotating real-world time series with LLMs. Building on this, we propose Context as Covariates (CoCo), a forecasting framework in which an LLM distills its textual reasoning into numerically grounded forecast and confidence covariates that guide a TSFM backbone, thereby exploiting the strengths of both LLMs and TSFMs. We align the two models in stages, first fine-tuning the LLM via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a reward tied to covariate-induced improvement in forecasting performance, and then fine-tuning the TSFM on covariates generated by the aligned LLM. Our experiments show that models trained on our synthetic multimodal corpus generalize to real benchmarks spanning finance, economics, weather, traffic, and security. Notably, CoCo with Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 as the LLM and Chronos2 as the TSFM achieves up to 12% MAE improvement over state-of-the-art TSFMs and LLM forecasters, such as GPT-4o. Code, checkpoints, and the evaluation datasets are publicly available.

Dynamic Strategy Selection

动态策略选择

We consider the problem of dynamically selecting among competing strategies using observable predictors. To mitigate estimation noise and excessive switching, we regularise the estimated policy with a switching cost, which may exceed actual transaction costs when these are too small to provide sufficient regularisation. Since switching costs make current choices affect future decisions, we formulate the problem using dynamic programming. The optimal policy is estimated using a reinforcement learning algorithm based on iterated regressions, making the procedure simple to implement. We apply the method to monthly rotation among momentum, value, profitability, and defensive U.S. equity factors over a sample of about 60 years. Using a parsimonious set of state variables, the estimated policy outperforms both the myopic dynamic policy and the best fixed factor in hindsight in out-of-sample evaluation. Under weak conditions, we show theoretically that the decision rule estimated from empirical data is asymptotically consistent for the unknown optimal decision rule. Simulation results illustrate the finite-sample relevance of these asymptotic results.

Hard to Fool: A Bounded LLM Research Loop for Quantitative Finance, and What Two Dozen Campaigns Found

难以愚弄：量化金融的有界LLM研究循环及两打实验的发现

Agentic research loops that let a language model propose a change, run a fixed experiment and keep whatever improves the metric work well in machine learning. In trading research the same loop is a machine for producing false discoveries: a backtest that "improves the metric" is, by default, overfit, look-ahead, a multiple-comparisons artifact, cost-free, or market drift wearing a strategy. We describe Autonomous Quant Researcher, an open-source loop built on the opposite premise: the model proposes, and only trusted code decides. Proposals are strict declarative JSON over a fixed vocabulary, so there is no model-written code to sandbox. Evaluation runs on immutable chronological stores split into discovery, validation and a final holdout that can be opened exactly once, after a durable receipt is written. Acceptance is a conjunction of absolute gates (positive edge and net P&L after costs, a positive weekly-cluster bootstrap lower bound, survival at elevated costs, per-year stability and sample floors), never a single score. Duplicates are refused before they spend a trial, and every formulation, refusal, crash and result is kept in a hash-chained, append-only ledger. We report every campaign run to date, across 0DTE options priced from traded option bars, overnight SPX verticals, daily ETF rules, technical-analysis premises, Kalshi crypto binaries, earnings options, off-exchange volume and a pre-registered alternative-data campaign. Most hypotheses fail, and we report them. Two results survive their pre-registered bars. The clearest example of the loop working is a failure: a language-model survivor that passed discovery and validation with Sharpe ratios near 3 and fell to 0.26 on its single holdout use. The value of such a loop lies less in what it finds than in how hard it is to make it report something that is not there.

核心方法： Autonomous Quant Researcher is an open-source research loop that uses strict declarative JSON proposals evaluated by trusted code. It employs immutable chronological data splits (discovery, validation, holdout) and multiple acceptance criteria (positive edge, net P&L, bootstrap lower bound, cost survival, per-year stability, sample floors) to prevent overfitting and false discoveries.

Selective Prediction Intervals: Jointly Learning to Predict, Bound, and Abstain

选择性预测区间：联合学习预测、边界和弃权

Many high-stakes regression decisions ask three things of a model at once: a point estimate to act on, an interval that bounds the risk of acting, and the option to abstain when neither can be trusted. Deep learning provides these in isolation: prediction-interval methods return an interval, and at best a value estimate inside it, for every input, while selective-prediction methods learn when to abstain, but only for point predictions and with no statement of how far the truth may lie from the estimate. We introduce SelPI, which trains interval, value and abstention heads jointly under a single loss with an explicit acceptance-rate budget. A natural objection is that interval width already tells us when to abstain. We show that it does not: across 18 tabular benchmarks, width predicts large point errors well (AUROC 0.72) but is anti-correlated with interval misses (AUROC 0.38), so abstaining on wide intervals discards precisely the inputs the interval got right. SelPI therefore selects with two gates, one trained on the interval loss and one on the value loss, coupled by a Fr'echet penalty that ties their separate budgets to the joint acceptance rate they deliver. With the base predictor held fixed, the learned gate beats width thresholding on a proper interval score on all 18 tabular datasets, closing a median 87% of the gap to an oracle selector, and it also beats selection by deep-ensemble disagreement on every dataset. The advantage grows with the heteroscedasticity of the task, and we report the settings in which it disappears.

核心方法： SelPI introduces a joint learning framework for prediction intervals, point estimates, and abstention decisions. It combines interval, value, and abstention heads under a single loss function with an explicit acceptance-rate budget. The method uses two trained gates (one for interval loss and one for value loss) coupled by a Fr'echet penalty to manage the joint acceptance rate.
数据来源： The paper evaluates the method on 18 tabular benchmarks, which could be analogous to financial datasets where heteroscedasticity and prediction uncertainty are common.

Regret Bounds for Market Making with Persistent Orders

持久订单做市的遗憾边界

We study adversarial market making with orders that remain active until execution. The maker operates on two markets: a primary market where orders are made and a secondary market where the resulting positions are hedged. In each round, the maker posts a quote on the primary market before observing both primary and secondary market prices. An order executes when the primary price reaches its quote, and the resulting position is immediately offset at the secondary price. When both price sequences are chosen by an adversary over $T$ rounds, we give a simple deterministic policy with regret of order $\log T$ against the best fixed quote (static comparator) and $\sqrt T$ against the best quote for each submission date (dynamic comparator), plus two terms measuring exposure to future primary and secondary prices. These rates remain optimal even when both exposure terms vanish and lower bounds show that the exposure terms are necessary. Our policy naturally extends to simultaneous bids and asks, where we show that static and dynamic regret are both characterized (up to constant factors) by the exposures terms (where the primary price exposure $V_T^{\mathrm{two}}$ now accounts for both bids and asks) and a term of order $\sqrt{V_T^{\mathrm{two}}\log T}$. Our policy is simple, efficient and fully parameterless. We extend this setting by demonstrating how natural assumptions on the price processes lead to improved guarantees without changing the policy.

核心方法： The paper presents a deterministic policy for market making with persistent orders, focusing on regret minimization against both static and dynamic comparators. The approach involves posting quotes on a primary market and hedging positions on a secondary market, with performance guarantees under adversarial price sequences.

FaCoDiff: Factor-Correlated Diffusion for Multivariate Probabilistic Forecasting

FaCoDiff: 多变量概率预测中的因子相关扩散方法

Multivariate probabilistic forecasting requires modeling cross-variable dependence alongside marginal uncertainty. The challenge is to learn this dependence while controlling marginal noise scales and avoiding dense covariance costs. We propose Factor-Correlated Diffusion (FaCoDiff), which couples a compact conditional statistical estimator with structured denoising. History-conditioned factor loadings use distinct coefficients for forecast-center correction, scale adjustment and noise dependence. Diagonal compensation preserves the estimated marginal noise variances, while low-rank operators enable residual Gaussian likelihood evaluation and noise transport without dense covariance storage or factorization. Factor-derived feature modulation explicitly conditions the denoiser, and a geometry-aware objective accounts for errors after noise transport. The same conditional geometry governs forward perturbations and reverse sampling. Across nine datasets, FaCoDiff achieves the lowest mean variogram score on eight datasets and ranks among the top two on 43 of 54 dataset-metric combinations in the reported comparisons. Matched ablations on Solar and Weather show that factor covariance improves mean Energy and Variogram scores under all four combinations of center and scale correction. Disrupting cross-variable sample pairings while preserving marginal trajectories worsens joint and aggregate scores on both datasets, demonstrating the predictive value of generated cross-variable dependence.

Generative Modeling for Cascading Extremes via Autoregressive Transformers

通过自回归变换器进行级联极值的生成建模

Extreme events often occur sequentially, from earthquake-tsunami cascades to successive extreme losses in financial markets. We introduce an extremal token representation that encodes multivariate observations as a categorical sequence. A quiet token records the absence of threshold exceedances; otherwise, the token identifies the exceeding component with the largest marginal probability-scale score. The representation is invariant to componentwise strictly increasing transformations. It preserves the identity and temporal ordering of dominant exceedances but discards their magnitudes and the identities of nondominant simultaneous exceedances. A decoder-only transformer estimates the history-dependent next-token kernel on this representation, and recursive sampling generates future token sequences from the induced joint predictive law. We amortize our generative model by pretraining across simulated process families with varying parameters and dependence persistence. Next-token predictions are evaluated against exact conditional probabilities for held-out categorical processes and particle filter references for heavy-tailed vector autoregressive processes. Applied to ten-second returns for five semiconductor stocks, the pretrained model supports zero-shot transfer, while a few epochs of adaptation improve agreement between observed and generated token frequencies and transition patterns. These results support task-specific tokenization and simulation-based pretraining for modeling which dimensions dominate multivariate extremes.

AdaMamba: Adaptive Frequency-Gated Mamba for Long-Term Time Series Forecasting

AdaMamba：用于长期时间序列预测的自适应频率门控Mamba模型

Time-series forecasting is foundational to a wide spectrum of web-scale services, spanning personalized recommendations, energy scheduling, and infrastructure capacity planning. Accurate long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) requires models to capture long-range temporal dependencies while adapting to periodic patterns that vary across variables. Although frequency-aware methods offer a global view of temporal dynamics, they typically rely on inflexible heuristic designs, such as predefined period lengths, manually selected or randomly sampled top-k frequency components, and separate spectral transformations. Consequently, they fail to account for time-frequency heterogeneity, where variables that appear synchronized in the time domain can differ substantially in the frequency domain. Meanwhile, Mamba-based forecasters, despite their efficiency in long-range modeling, introduce frequency information only as auxiliary signals, leaving spectral evidence disconnected from state transitions. In this work, we propose AdaMamba, a novel approach that explicitly internalizes input-dependent frequency information into the modeling of temporal dependencies. AdaMamba comprises an interaction learning module and an adaptive frequency-gated state space module: the former captures cross-variable dependencies and multi-scale temporal context, while the latter generates context-dependent frequency bases and unifies temporal forgetting into a single time-frequency gate that directly modulates state evolution. Extensive experiments on seven public LTSF benchmarks and four domain-specific datasets demonstrate that AdaMamba consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines while maintaining competitive computational efficiency.

核心方法： AdaMamba combines an interaction learning module for cross-variable dependencies and multi-scale temporal context with an adaptive frequency-gated state space module that integrates frequency information into state evolution, enabling dynamic handling of time-frequency heterogeneity in long-term time series forecasting.

LLM Agents Already Know When to Call Tools -- Even Without Reasoning

LLM代理已经知道何时调用工具——甚至无需推理

Tool-augmented LLM agents tend to call tools indiscriminately, even when the model can answer directly. Each unnecessary call wastes API fees and latency, yet no existing benchmark systematically studies when a tool call is actually needed. We propose When2Tool, a benchmark of 18 environments (15 single-hop, 3 multi-hop) spanning three categories of tool necessity -- computational scale, knowledge boundaries, and execution reliability -- each with controlled difficulty levels that create a clear decision boundary between tool-necessary and tool-unnecessary tasks. We evaluate two families of training-free baselines: Prompt-only (varying the prompt to discourage unnecessary calls) and Reason-then-Act (requiring the model to reason about tool necessity before acting). Both provide limited control: Prompt-only suppresses necessary calls alongside unnecessary ones, and Reason-then-Act still incurs a disproportionate accuracy cost on hard tasks. To understand why these baselines fail, we probe the models' hidden states and find that tool necessity is linearly decodable from the pre-generation representation with AUROC 0.89--0.96 across six models, substantially exceeding the model's own verbalized reasoning. This reveals that models already know when tools are needed, but fail to act on this knowledge during generation. Building on this finding, we propose Probe&Prefill, which uses a lightweight linear probe to read the hidden-state signal and prefills the model's response with a steering sentence. Across all models tested, Probe&Prefill reduces tool calls by 38\% with only 1.7\% accuracy loss, while the best baseline at comparable accuracy only reduces 6% of tool calls, or achieves a similar tool call reduction but incurs a 5$\times$ higher accuracy loss. Our code is available at https://github.com/Trustworthy-ML-Lab/when2tool

Epiplexity Guided Data Selection and Generation for Out-of-Distribution Generalization

基于复杂性指导的数据选择与生成以实现分布外泛化

Modern systems are increasingly expected to transfer across tasks not specified during training. What data facilitates generalization to unanticipated settings? One hypothesis is that data with more structural information could contain shared circuits and subprograms that may be recycled in a wider array of downstream settings. Epiplexity, a recently proposed measure of the structural information a compute-bounded learner can extract from data, offers a mechanism to reason about this relationship. As existing estimators for epiplexity only operate on data already seen during training, we show how to operationalize epiplexity as an online training signal by greedily maximizing its stepwise gain in two settings. For data selection, we fit scaling laws to the training loss curves of natural data domains to predict the expected epiplexity gain as a function of training tokens, and use this signal to adaptively determine the sampling weights over domains during training. For synthetic data generation, where no domain structure exists, we reward a generator with the change in epiplexity of a learner's synthetic training data and use REINFORCE policy gradients to guide the generator toward an epiplexity-maximizing distribution. In both cases, we find higher epiplexity predicts improved downstream performance on zero-shot and fine-tuning tasks, supporting the hypothesis that data rich in structural information yield representations that better transfer across domains.
