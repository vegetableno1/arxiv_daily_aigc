Deep catastrophic-risk-aware reinforcement learning

深度灾难风险感知的强化学习

Estimating extreme risk in reinforcement learning becomes increasingly difficult as the confidence level increases, since only a small number of observations contribute to empirical estimates of Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR). In this paper, we extend an Extreme Value Theory (EVT) approach for CVaR optimization to high-dimensional neural-network policies. Using a peaks-over-threshold representation and method-of-moments estimation, we obtain a differentiable tail-risk objective and show that, conditional on the selected threshold, its finite-sample gradient can be computed directly through automatic differentiation. We first validate the approach in a controlled experiment with a known optimal solution and then investigate its performance in a deep-hedging problem with rare extreme market movements. Our results show similar hedging behavior for EVT- and empirical-CVaR-trained policies under normal conditions, while EVT-trained policies exhibit lower hedging losses under the extreme-scenario evaluation across the training sample sizes considered. These results suggest that EVT can provide a practical alternative for training neural policies when the observations relevant to extreme risk are scarce.

核心方法： Extends Extreme Value Theory (EVT) approach for Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) optimization to high-dimensional neural-network policies using a peaks-over-threshold representation and method-of-moments estimation, resulting in a differentiable tail-risk objective.
数据来源： Financial market data for deep-hedging problem with rare extreme market movements.

Universal Online Convex Optimization With Delayed Feedback

具有延迟反馈的通用在线凸优化

We study universal online convex optimization with delayed feedback. We develop a single algorithm that simultaneously adapts to general convex, strongly convex, and exp-concave losses without prior knowledge of the curvature class or parameters. Our regret bounds match the best known dependence on the time horizon $T$ and delays up to doubly logarithmic factors: roughly $\sqrt{T + d_{\mathrm{tot}}}$ for convex losses and $\sigma_{\max}\log T$ rates with curved losses, where $d_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is the total cumulative delay and $\sigma_{\max}$ is the maximum number of simultaneously missing observations. Our main technical contribution is a delayed meta-learner with a second-order excess-loss guarantee, which enables curvature-adaptive aggregation of delayed base learners. In addition, our new exp-concave base learner yields a sharper regret bound which improves the state-of-the-art delay dependence by replacing the maximum delay with the potentially much smaller $\sigma_{\max}$, while retaining the complementary $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{tot}}}$ dependence.

核心方法： Universal online convex optimization algorithm that adapts to different loss curvature (convex, strongly convex, exp-concave) without prior knowledge of curvature parameters, using delayed feedback aggregation with second-order excess-loss guarantees

LOB-Impact Benchmark: Emergent Market Impact in Generative Limit Order Book Models

LOB-Impact Benchmark: 生成限价订单簿模型中的涌现市场影响

Realistic market impact is essential for generative limit order book (LOB) models to serve as world models for trading agents. If a simulator misprices this mechanism, agents learn policies that are optimal in the simulator but wrong in the real market. Existing metrics assess generated streams purely through distributional comparison with historical data, leaving counterfactual response to an agent's own orders largely unexplored. Here, we show that market impact emerges naturally in neural LOB generators trained solely for next-token prediction. We inject execution-style metaorders into the generated message flow of five autoregressive models across five NASDAQ stocks and measure the response using estimators from the empirical impact literature, validated on baselines whose physics is known in advance. Our benchmark evaluates any message-level generator in three phases: free generation (reading order-flow persistence against price diffusivity); metaorder injection (yielding the impact law's exponent and amplitude); and a post-execution window (measuring relaxation). In addition, comparing each visible injection to a blind, simulator-only control isolates the model's learned response from standard book mechanics. For every model on every stock, we measure how the impact responds as a function of cumulative metaorder participation. Interestingly, we observe emergent impact in generative models of market data, with exponents in the range of 0.63 to 1.21, slightly above the traditional 0.5 of the square-root law of impact. In the family of models tested, we are so far unable to observe impact decay during relaxation. We tentatively attribute this to the still-short context windows of the models. We release the benchmark as open source to validate future generative models for market data.

Covariance-Aware Transformers for Quadratic Programming and Decision Making

用于二次规划和决策的协方差感知Transformer

We explore the use of transformers for solving quadratic programs and how this capability benefits decision-making problems that involve covariance matrices. We first show that the linear attention mechanism can provably solve unconstrained QPs by tokenizing the matrix variables (e.g.~$A$ of the objective $\frac{1}{2}x^\top Ax+b^\top x$) row-by-row and emulating gradient descent iterations. Furthermore, by incorporating MLPs, a transformer block can solve (i) $\ell_1$-penalized QPs by emulating iterative soft-thresholding and (ii) $\ell_1$-constrained QPs when equipped with an additional feedback loop. Our theory motivates us to introduce Time2Decide: a generic method that enhances a time series foundation model (TSFM) by explicitly feeding the covariance matrix between the variates. We empirically find that Time2Decide uniformly outperforms the base TSFM model for the classical portfolio optimization problem that admits an $\ell_1$-constrained QP formulation. Remarkably, Time2Decide also outperforms the classical "Predict-then-Optimize (PtO)" procedure, where we first forecast the returns and then explicitly solve a constrained QP, in suitable settings. Our results demonstrate that transformers benefit from explicit use of second-order statistics, and this can enable them to effectively solve complex decision-making problems, like portfolio construction, in one forward pass.

The central hypothesis is that incorporating market regime information into the NCO pipeline, specifically at the stages of covariance estimation, hierarchical clustering, and weight optimization, yields portfolios with improved risk-adjusted performance and reduced downside risk relative to regime-agnostic baselines. Regime-conditional covariance matrices and clustering structures parameterize NCO at each rebalancing event. An adaptive rebalancing rule, triggered by regime transitions or material changes in optimal weights, replaces fixed-calendar rebalancing. The framework is evaluated on a heterogeneous monthly multi-asset universe comprising Indonesian government bonds (SBN), the LQ45 equity index, gold (IDR-denominated), foreign exchange (CNY/IDR), and Bitcoin/IDR (BTC). Performance metrics include cumulative return, annualized Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio, maximum drawdown, Calmar ratio, turnover, and net-of-cost returns. Statistical significance is assessed using the Jobson-Korkie test. Financial institutions increasingly deploy large language models (LLMs) in multilingual markets, raising a practical question: can pipelines developed and validated in English be reused across languages, or must they be rebuilt per locale? We test cross-lingual reusability along three dimensions--accuracy, reasoning-effort transfer, and behavioural stability--through three complementary tasks. Task 1 (cheque extraction) and Task 2 (Mintaka QA with pinned evidence across nine languages and nine model-effort configurations) test whether accuracy and effort settings transfer; Task 3 (FinanceBench pilot, 50 items over SEC 10-K filings in four languages) validates whether these patterns generalise to financial content. Accuracy reusability is broadly confirmed: Gemini 3.5 Flash achieves 95.2-97.6% extraction accuracy across prompt languages, and FinanceBench per-language means span 5.8pp (77.1-82.9%), though the language-difficulty ranking reverses between domains (Hindi is worst on Mintaka, best on FinanceBench at 82.9%). Effort reusability is domain-dependent: higher reasoning effort yields marginal gains on general-knowledge QA but up to 8pp on financial content, and effort configurations that help in English help proportionally in other languages. Behavioural reusability holds: citation-set overlap with English averages 77-79% Jaccard, with no single language consistently worse. Together, these results support qualified reuse of English-built pipelines, but per-domain, per-language validation remains necessary--neither semantic similarity nor self-reported confidence reliably predicts cross-lingual accuracy.

Training-Free Continual Learning for Multimodal Forecasting via Prompt Evolution

通过提示进化实现无需训练的多模态预测持续学习

Adapting a forecasting system to a changing world classically means retraining. We ask whether the task of continually learning regimes for time series forecasting can be represented by a piece of text: a guidance block, evolved by outcome-driven reflection and used in a frozen LLM forecaster's prompt. On a refreshed multimodal benchmark (weekly search indices, daily currencies, daily commodities, each window paired with web-retrieved news), we run GEPA-style prompt evolution under strict contamination control to prevent the data leakage common in timeseries benchmarks: the forecaster's knowledge cutoff predates every evaluated window. A 900-word evolved guidance carried by Llama-3.3-70B beats TimeSeries Foundation Models (TSFMs) on held-out data (GM MASE 0.776 vs. 0.898 for TabPFN-TS-3), transfers to more recent models without measurable loss, and costs dollars rather than GPU-days to update. We characterize the loop: out-of-loop gains concentrate in the first reflection, in-loop improvements overstate out-of-loop gains 2-3x unless an untouched monitor arbitrates, and error-only objectives silently suppress the text modality. We provide two extensions. First, compiling the guidance into executable code with a TSFM as a callable tool recovers most of the gain deterministically. Second, ablations show the advantage widens at short horizons and that removing the news text does not hurt the guided forecaster, consistent with the text's value being distilled into policy during learning. We argue that continual prompt evolution is a practical, training-free alternative to retraining for multimodal forecasting.

OptScientist: Automated Discovery of Optimization Laws

OptScientist：优化定律的自动化发现

Many scientific phenomena are best understood as outcomes of underlying optimization problems, yet automated scientific discovery has largely focused on recovering equations that describe observed input-output patterns. We introduce OptScientist, a novel automated framework for discovering compact symbolic optimization laws directly from data. OptScientist comprises (i) a model that infers symbolic optimization problems by first learning to represent data as an optimization problem and then extracting its symbolic form, and (ii) an LLM agentic system embedding the model in a discovery process that integrates theoretical priors from scientific literature, discovers candidate laws, abstracts their underlying mechanisms, and suggests new data to collect. We first evaluate OptScientist on OptFeynman, a novel benchmark of 99 physics-derived optimization problems, where OptScientist recovers ground-truth laws for over 80% of the problems and remains robust to noisy contextual information, while substantially outperforming machine-learning, symbolic, and LLM-based model-discovery baselines. On human risky choice and central-bank monetary policy, OptScientist discovers compact symbolic optimization laws that achieve held-out predictive performance competitive with leading baselines, while using few parameters and substantially fewer tokens than LLM baselines. Overall, OptScientist extends automated scientific discovery to uncovering optimization laws that generate observed phenomena, enabling automated theory building across the physical, biological, behavioral, and social sciences.

核心方法： OptScientist combines symbolic optimization discovery with LLM agentic systems to infer compact optimization laws from data, integrating theoretical priors and suggesting new data collection. It includes learning to represent data as optimization problems and extracting their symbolic forms.
数据来源： Human risky choice data and central-bank monetary policy data, as well as physics-derived optimization problems for benchmarking.
