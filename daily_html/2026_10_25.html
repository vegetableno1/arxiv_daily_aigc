Learning What to Share: Expert Specialization for Time-Series Forecasting

学习共享内容：时序预测中的专家专业化

A central challenge in multivariate forecasting is deciding what to share across channels and where to specialize. Mixture-of-experts models offer flexible sharing, yet it remains unclear how much of their forecasting gains comes from added capacity or expert allocation. Through controlled comparisons across eleven datasets and three backbones, we show that informative channel assignments improve forecasting beyond matched shared capacity and random allocation. On frozen transformer backbones, persistent channel-specific mixtures outperform input-dependent routing on average. To examine when and how much specialization helps, we introduce GalaxyTS, a controlled synthetic dataset with exact shared and channel-specific forecasting references. It reveals how persistent channel mixtures exploit predictive differences concealed by short input windows and measures gains over matched shared corrections. These insights motivate Persistent Closed-Form Routing (PCR), a plug-and-play method that alternates exact conditional estimation of channel-specific mixture weights with gradient-based training of low-rank residual experts around a frozen pretrained forecaster. Averaged over four forecasting horizons, PCR-K4 achieves dataset-balanced mean squared error reductions of 3.86-14.25% relative to the three frozen backbones. In matched comparisons, PCR outperforms competing specialization methods on average while requiring substantially fewer trainable parameters.

核心方法： The paper introduces Persistent Closed-Form Routing (PCR), a plug-and-play method that alternates exact conditional estimation of channel-specific mixture weights with gradient-based training of low-rank residual experts around a frozen pretrained forecaster. This approach is designed to improve multivariate time-series forecasting by optimizing the sharing and specialization of information across channels.
数据来源： The methodology is evaluated across eleven datasets, including the synthetic GalaxyTS dataset designed to reveal predictive differences between shared and channel-specific forecasting references. Optimal portfolio choice under green transition timing risk

绿色转型时机风险下的最优投资组合选择

We study an investor's optimal portfolio choice problem when they account for the impact of a possible future green transition, whose timing is uncertain. We model the green transition as a discontinuity in the stock price processes, driven by a single jump process with constant intensity, in which 'green' stocks increase in value and 'brown' stocks decrease in value. The investor is ambiguous about the timing of the green transition and designs an investment strategy which is robust to this risk. The investor has constant relative risk aversion preferences over both wealth and model ambiguity, which introduce a multiplicative ambiguity penalty for power utility and an additive ambiguity penalty for log utility. We separate the problem into pre- and post-transition cases, derive the solution, and provide a verification theorem. We further show that the log utility, ambiguity-neutral, and worst-case problems are obtained as special cases of our general problem. In a case study with one green and one brown stock, we show that when the stock prices are positively correlated, an investor who is aware of transition risk increases their investment in the green stock and reduces their investment in the brown stock, compared to when they ignore the possibility of a transition. We illustrate the difference between an ambiguity-averse and a risk-averse investor, and show how an ambiguity-averse investor re-allocates their portfolio as their view on the probability of a green transition changes.

核心方法： The paper presents a portfolio optimization framework that accounts for the uncertainty in the timing of a green transition, modeled as a discontinuous jump in stock prices. It employs robust optimization techniques to handle ambiguity in the transition timing, using constant relative risk aversion preferences for both wealth and model ambiguity.
数据来源： Theoretical framework with potential application to real-world stock data, specifically green and brown stocks. The case study suggests using historical and simulated data for green and brown stocks with positively correlated prices.
Alpha 潜力：

Performance Gaps Between Open-Loop, Certainty-Equivalent, and Optimal Control Under Action-Dependent Observations

开环控制、确定性等价与最优控制在动作相关观测下的性能差距研究

In dynamical systems, feedback can improve performance by allowing decisions to depend on past observations, rather than being designed a priori. Under full observation, the current state is sufficient for decision-making; under partial observation, the controller must instead estimate the state from measurements. When actions also affect observations, control can have a dual role: actions influence both the state and the information available for future decisions. This interaction can invalidate certainty-equivalent (CE) control, a widely used strategy that applies state feedback policies as if the estimated state were the true state. We ask when this suboptimality leads to a quantitative performance gap that grows with the horizon. We study this question in a linear-quadratic system with bilinear observations by comparing the optimal policy, certainty-equivalent control, and the optimal open-loop (OL) policy. We identify an instance-dependent information-strength parameter $q$ and establish distinct performance regimes as $q$ varies. When $q$ is sufficiently small, we show that no policy can improve over OL control by more than $O(1)$ cumulative cost. For large values of $q$, we find that CE achieves an $Ω(T)$ advantage over OL control. Our main finding is that for intermediate values of $q$, we can construct a policy that occasionally probes for information and achieves an $Ω(T)$ advantage over OL control while CE only achieves an $O(1)$ advantage, establishing an $Ω(T)$ gap between CE and optimal control. Our analysis relies on a regret decomposition that captures the tradeoff between the immediate cost of gathering information and its future benefit.

核心方法： The paper examines performance differences between open-loop, certainty-equivalent, and optimal control in dynamical systems with action-dependent observations. It employs a linear-quadratic framework with bilinear observations to quantify the information-value tradeoff in sequential decision-making.
数据来源： Theoretical framework applicable to any time-series data where actions influence future observations (e.g., trading volumes moving prices)
Alpha 潜力：

Significant for active trading strategies where execution impacts market information (e.g., optimal trade scheduling). The Ω(T) advantage of optimal probing over CE control suggests potential gains in high-frequency trading scenarios where actions measurably affect price discovery.

Faithful Federated Tabular Data Synthesis

可信的联邦表格数据合成

Synthetic data is an important data source in the AI era. This paper studies the generation of mixed-type tabular synthetic data in federated settings, where two key challenges arise: ensuring distributional fidelity and effectively handling mixed-type data. We propose Federated Integrative Two-Step Synthesis (FITS), a framework for mixed-type tabular data synthesis. At its core is a two-step federated algorithm for estimating conditional cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) using local linear regression. Building on this algorithm, we develop a federated synthesis procedure based on the probability chain rule to ensure distributional fidelity. To handle mixed-type data, FITS introduces a continualization method that lifts discrete variables into a continuous space and recovers their discrete distributions without loss at both the density-function and CDF levels. This transformation provides a unified continuous representation of mixed-type variables, facilitating the application of local linear regression and synthetic data generation. Our theoretical analysis characterizes the conditional density and cumulative distribution functions of the continualized mixed-type variables and establishes the uniform convergence of the resulting federated conditional CDF estimators. Experiments demonstrate that FITS-generated synthetic data improves downstream task performance for all clients, with the benefits depending on how the synthetic data is used.

Tracking High-Frequency Trading Activity in China and Its Effects on Market Quality

跟踪中国高频交易活动及其对市场质量的影响

We develop a new order-level proxy for high-frequency trading (HFT) activity based on precise order placement and study its effects on market quality. With quotes updating multiple times per second, millisecond-level order-book access makes high-frequency traders more likely to submit active exact-match orders, defined as limit orders priced exactly at the prevailing best opposite-side quote. Using Shenzhen Stock Exchange data, we validate the proxy: active exact-match orders respond faster to quote updates, cancel more rapidly, concentrate when depth imbalance is favorable and spreads are narrow, and predict mid-quote returns from 100 milliseconds through ten minutes. A difference-in-differences design exploiting cross-channel variation in order dissemination latency at the market open shows that lower latency increases active exact-match shares. This variation yields plausibly causal evidence that latency-induced HFT raises short-horizon price variation, wide-spread incidence, and adverse-selection exposure, while realized spreads decline and pricing error falls modestly. Unlike developed markets, China's T+1 constraint, absence of maker-taker rebates, and limits on rapid quote revision tilt speed advantages toward directional liquidity taking, highlighting how market design shapes the channel through which speed affects market quality.

核心方法： The paper introduces an order-level proxy for high-frequency trading (HFT) activity based on active exact-match orders, which are limit orders priced exactly at the prevailing best opposite-side quote. The methodology includes validation of the proxy using millisecond-level order-book data and a difference-in-differences design to study the effects of latency on HFT activity and market quality.
数据来源： Shenzhen Stock Exchange data, including millisecond-level order-book updates and order dissemination latency at market open.

TempShap: When and How Much a Feature Matters in Time-Series Forecasting

TempShap：时间序列预测中特征何时以及如何起作用

Attribution for a time-series forecaster should answer two questions that a feature-level scalar cannot: when in the input window a feature acts, and how much its influence changes across the forecast horizon. Most existing Shapley explainers treat each feature as one player and therefore collapse both axes. We propose TempShap, a retrospective, loss-based Shapley framework whose players are the individual input cells $(t,i)$. Its attribution tensor yields temporal-localization and horizon-impact profiles. We validate these against planted temporal structures, a tractable exact cell game, and a closed-form attribution oracle for a real linear forecaster. Permutation sharing estimates the cell-level game at localized perturbation cost. Under redundant inputs, one-at-a-time scores under-credit substitutable information, whereas the signed Shapley decomposition preserves total value and yields more reliable held-out deletion rankings under mean masking. A planted counterexample isolates the redundancy mechanism. Experiments show a deletion-ranking advantage across Shapley estimators, forecasting benchmarks, and model families. TempShap therefore makes fine-grained Shapley attribution both practical and operationally useful.

核心方法： A temporal Shapley value framework (TempShap) for feature attribution in time-series forecasting, focusing on when and how much each feature contributes across time windows and forecast horizons. Incorporates permutation sharing for efficient computation and handles redundant inputs through signed Shapley decomposition.
数据来源： Generic time-series forecasting data (could be applied to financial time series - e.g., price, volume, or alternative data streams)

Graph-dependent pooling: adaptive granularity in hierarchical graph neural networks

图依赖池化：层次图神经网络中的自适应粒度

Graph Neural Networks are widely used to learn structural representations of relational data. Hierarchical graph pooling learns multi-level representations by progressively coarsening graphs; however, prior methods rely on predefined cluster numbers or fixed pooling ratios, limiting adaptability to individual graphs. We propose Graph-Dependent Pooling (GDPool), which dynamically determines pooling granularity for each graph. We analyze optimization conflicts among layer-wise objectives through gradient alignment, showing that independently supervised pooling levels can send conflicting gradients to shared parameters. GDPool uses self-attention to select graph-specific anchors representing structurally important nodes and constructs coarse graphs through soft node assignment. Experiments on metal-organic framework and molecular graph benchmarks show that GDPool consistently achieves the best predictive performance across all benchmarks. Evaluations using modularity and the effective number of clusters show that GDPool achieves consistently higher modularity than existing methods while the number of active clusters decreases monotonically with depth. Sensitivity analyses confirm stable behavior across hyperparameter and architectural choices.

核心方法： Graph-Dependent Pooling (GDPool) is a hierarchical graph neural network technique that dynamically adjusts pooling granularity for each graph using self-attention to select structurally important nodes and soft node assignment for graph coarsening. An Empirical Study of Reinforcement Learning Scaling Laws

强化学习缩放定律的实证研究

The discovery of scaling laws enables prediction of the performance, the optimal resource allocation strategy, and the best hyperparameter configuration of a large-scale training job based on inexpensive experiments at smaller scales. Guided by these empirical scaling laws, the deep learning community has achieved great success in training large (self-)supervised models on Internet-scale datasets. In contrast, despite its potential and wide application, reinforcement learning (RL) has yet to find a guiding principle for predictable scaling. In this work, we conduct an empirical study of RL scaling laws of an actor-critic algorithm in two procedurally generated environments, Starpilot and Craftax. We discover empirical scaling laws with strong predictive performance in both environments. The discovered scaling laws accurately predict the aforementioned three quantities for larger-scale training jobs up to two orders of magnitude in total training compute. In particular, we employ a variant of Elo scores for the single-agent RL paradigm as an alternative performance metric than the commonly used mean episode return. Our results show that the Elo score is a much more predictable metric than the mean episode return, making it a better choice for fitting scaling laws. By applying our discovered scaling laws to increase training compute, we establish a new state of the art in the challenging Craftax environment. Beneath the surface of the global scaling trend, we also observe a non-monotonic scaling pattern of the compute-optimal model size in Craftax. We connect this observation to a pathology known as Ray interference.

LSTM-Style Looped Transformers with Shared KV Caches

具有共享KV缓存的LSTM风格循环Transformer

Recurrent transformers reuse weights to perform deep computation with fewer parameters. We study an LSTM-style transformer that also shares one key--value cache across its recurrent core. At width 1024, it uses 40.4% fewer parameters than an untied control at nearly matched forward FLOPs, and reduces persistent cache sets from fifteen to four. Parallel training supplies cache entries from a standard-attention pass, whereas continuation reads entries produced recursively using shared attention. We address this mismatch by aligning the two training passes' cache entries with a consistency loss during the final 10% of a fixed training-token budget. Across three model widths on FineWeb, this removes 81--89% of the continuation-loss penalty relative to full-cache evaluation. The improvement replicates on disjoint documents for two training seeds. Across four widths and 1.05--4.19B training tokens, the loss cost of weight tying is smallest at lower token budgets, suggesting an opportunity to use recurrent computation when data and model storage are limited.

The Clustering Occupation Kernel (CLOCK) Method for Time Series Arising from Dynamical Systems

动态系统时间序列的聚类占据核（CLOCK）方法

We introduce the Clustering Occupation Kernel (CLOCK) method, a novel representation-based framework for clustering time series arising from dynamical systems. CLOCK embeds observed sequences into a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) via a time-averaged occupation kernel, enabling trajectory comparison through their RKHS norm. For systems governed by differential equations, this formulation rigorously approximates the kernel mean embedding of the invariant Sinai-Ruelle-Bowen (SRB) measure, meaning our dissimilarity metric approximates the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) between attractors. To accommodate general time series, we introduce time lags via Takens' Embedding Theorem to reconstruct the phase space. Crucially, CLOCK natively handles trajectories of unequal lengths and irregularly-spaced observations. We validate the proposed metric using spectral clustering, demonstrating superior performance against benchmark methods on both synthetic datasets and a real-world intracranial large-scale electroencephalogram (EEG) dataset. Finally, ablation studies on the Lorenz-96 system are consistent with the theoretical convergence rates of the occupation kernels and highlight the method's strong robustness to zero-mean Gaussian noise.

核心方法： The paper introduces the Clustering Occupation Kernel (CLOCK) method, which embeds time series data into a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) using a time-averaged occupation kernel. This allows for trajectory comparison through their RKHS norm, approximating the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) between attractors. The method handles trajectories of unequal lengths and irregularly-spaced observations, making it suitable for various time series data.

Reinforcement Learning for Controller Parameters in the Auto-Bidding System at Pinterest

Pinterest自动竞价系统中控制器参数的强化学习应用

Auto-bidding systems in online advertising must balance competing objectives-maximizing budget delivery while minimizing cost per action-across heterogeneous campaigns under non-stationary market conditions. This trade-off evolves over campaign lifespans: early on, cost optimization dominates when budgets roll over; near deadlines, budget delivery becomes urgent. While PID controllers are widely used in production for their stability and interpretability, fixed tunings cannot adapt to diverse campaigns and shifting traffic patterns. We present RLBP (Reinforcement Learning for Bidding Parameters), a hybrid architecture deployed at Pinterest that uses RL as a meta-controller to select campaign-specific PID parameters daily while retaining PID for high-frequency bid adjustments. RLBP trains a multi-head Q-network using a relative-lift reward formulation normalized against the production baseline, and performs inference-time fusion that adapts the cost-delivery trade-off to campaign states without retraining. Through online A/B testing at Pinterest, RLBP achieves a statistically significant reduction in effective cost-per-click (eCPC) with no revenue degradation, and two months of post-rollout measurements against a holdout also show consistent cost reductions. These results demonstrate that meta-controller RL can successfully augment production control systems at scale.

Distilling Expert Judgment at Scale

大规模专家判断提炼

Frontier AI models are increasingly deployed on topics where the stakes are high and expert judgment is required, including healthcare, defense, finance, governance, and politics. We present a general methodology for distilling expert human judgment into automated evaluation systems that operate at scale. Our approach assembles networks of domain experts and develops techniques for extracting not just their conclusions but their reasoning processes, encoding those processes into structured editorial standards that automated judges can apply continuously. We ground our approach in a long tradition of research on the nature of expertise and the conditions under which expert judgment is reliable and more effective than other approaches, and argue that independent, expert-calibrated evaluation is necessary not only on epistemic grounds but as a matter of accountability and public trust. We validate the methodology on AI news reporting as a first application domain. On source quality, two independent domain experts achieve Krippendorff's $α= 0.83/0.69$ on a held-out test set when applying the editorial standards, and our automated judge reaches $F1 = 0.81/0.97$ against expert consensus. On neutrality, experts achieve $α= .72$ on the test set, and the automated judge reaches $F1 = 0.86$ against expert consensus. The 26-feature logistic regression benefits from adding later features to past features, whereas replacing the past pair count with the later pair count reduces MRR by 0.157 on tgbl-wiki under exhaustive ranking. A control removing later interactions from query sources to historically observed destinations reduces the MRR gain by 59% on tgbl-wiki and 10% on tgbl-comment. Because this control removes more than repeated target pairs, these reductions cannot be attributed to pair recurrence alone. Later events improve the ranking of hidden interactions on both datasets, including tgbl-comment, where only 6.4% of target pairs reappeared within 24 hours.
