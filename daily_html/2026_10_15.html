What Do Foundation Models for Market Microstructure Learn? A Controlled Study with Synthetic Limit Order Books

市场微观结构基础模型学到了什么？基于合成限价订单簿的对照研究

Autoregressive transformers trained on limit order book (LOB) message streams are increasingly proposed as foundation models for market microstructure. Because the generative process of real markets is unknown, these models can be judged only by the realism of their output, leaving open what they actually learn. We make the question answerable by inverting the setup: we train GPT-style models on order flow from simulators whose latent parameters are known, so that the Bayes-optimal predictor, the generating state, and the exact data entropy serve as references. Measured against them, next-token pretraining on order flow implements amortized Bayesian inference over market regimes. The learned predictive matches the exact Bayes optimum to within a millinat per token and approaches the oracle floor even on a nonstationary family whose latent state drifts within an episode, a regime that requires filtering. The latent regime is linearly decodable from frozen activations, well above baselines, so a frozen-model read-out calibrates simulator parameters more accurately than windowed maximum likelihood at a fraction of the cost. In our experiments these capabilities are not produced by scale: capacity and token volume saturate early, and it is the diversity of training regimes, not the token count, that determines generalization to unseen markets. On real NASDAQ data, zero-shot transfer is set by how well the simulator prior covers real flow: an enriched mixture nearly closes the gap to an in-domain baseline, and pretraining beats from-scratch training at every budget across held-out tickers. Known ground truth turns average next-token loss, which is uninformative near the entropy floor, into an exact comparison against the Bayes-optimal and oracle predictors.

It focuses on how these models perform amortized Bayesian inference over market regimes, comparing their predictions against the Bayes-optimal predictor and oracle references. The study leverages known ground truth from simulators to evaluate model performance, including latent regime decoding and zero-shot transfer to real market data.

Data-Driven Dynamic Portfolio Selection with Vine Copula Simulation and Reinforcement Learning

基于藤Copula模拟与强化学习的数据驱动动态组合选择

We develop a data-driven approach to dynamic portfolio selection that combines vine-copula simulation with reinforcement learning. The investor has constant relative risk aversion and maximises expected terminal utility with a running penalty on the conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) of one-period losses. We estimate a conditional vine model from historical returns and use it to generate joint scenarios for CVaR evaluation and conditional paths for training a recurrent portfolio policy. Under uniform local derivative bounds, we propagate edgewise pair-copula parameter errors through the conditional transforms to obtain power-law stability bounds in Hellinger distance and total variation. In a finite-horizon investment model, we combine these estimates with a marginal and structural approximation budget to bound policy values, optimal values and the true-market loss of policies selected under a fitted model, with separate simulation and numerical optimisation terms. On a seven-asset panel comprising US and Chinese equities and gold, the selected ensemble of ten trained policies achieves a compound annual growth rate of 34.13% and a Sharpe ratio of 2.31 over 22 complete monthly holding periods, exceeding the corresponding values of six classical and vine-based benchmarks. Combining simulated and historical training data reduces monthly target-to-target turnover by 41.5% relative to training on historical data alone, while maintaining comparable growth.

核心方法： The paper combines vine-copula simulation for conditional returns estimation with reinforcement learning to train a recurrent portfolio policy. It incorporates CVaR penalty in the utility maximization framework and provides stability bounds for the model.
数据来源： Historical returns from a seven-asset panel comprising US and Chinese equities and gold.

Despite the large number of anomaly detection methods proposed in the literature, recent benchmark studies have shown that no single detector performs best across highly heterogeneous time series. GBTSVR: Twin Support Vector Regression via Granular Ball Computing

基于粒球计算的双支持向量回归(GBTSVR)

Regression models used in real-world applications are often challenged by noise, outliers, data heterogeneity, and asymmetric error characteristics, which severely limit the effectiveness of conventional point-wise learning models. While twin support vector regression (TSVR) partially addresses these issues through the use of nonparallel regression bounds, it still relies on individual samples as basic learning units. As a result, TSVR remains highly sensitive to noisy and contaminated observations and fails to leverage the underlying structural and regional information inherent in complex datasets, leading to suboptimal robustness and generalization performance. To address these limitations, we introduce granular ball twin support vector regression (GBTSVR) and its quantile extension, granular ball twin support vector quantile regression (GB-TSVQR). The proposed models integrate granular ball computing with TSVR, replacing individual data points with compact, structure-preserving granular balls as fundamental learning units. This region-level representation effectively suppresses noise and outliers while preserving intrinsic data geometry, thereby enhancing robustness, stability, and generalization. GBTSVR constructs nonparallel lower- and upper-bound regression functions over granular balls, significantly reducing sensitivity to sample-level perturbations. Building upon this framework, GB-TSVQR further incorporates quantile regression principles to simultaneously capture heterogeneous trends and asymmetric error distributions across different quantile levels. Extensive experiments on synthetic datasets, time-series benchmarks, and outlier-contaminated data demonstrate that the proposed GBTSVR and GB-TSVQR consistently outperform state-of-the-art regression models.

MorphingNet: Symbolic Regression Where the Network Is the Formula

MorphingNet: 以网络结构作为公式的符号回归方法

We introduce MorphingNet, a network for symbolic regression (SR) whose units change their function continuously. Instead of searching over expressions built from a fixed operator library, MorphingNet trains the form of each function as a parameter, so SR becomes one continuous optimization problem. Its units are either pole-free rational functions or complex exponential--logarithm functions, which move between exponentials, sinusoids, damped oscillations, power laws and logarithms as their parameters change. The network is a readable formula at every step of training, and no operator library is needed to train it; a structure-recovery step afterwards rewrites the trained formula into short elementary expressions. Training is dense linear algebra, so it is GPU-friendly and scales to large data. MorphingNet achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on SRBench, recovers most generating formulas on synthetic benchmarks, and on large data is up to two orders of magnitude faster than the strongest search method. By turning the search for a formula into the training of a network, MorphingNet offers a new paradigm for SR and for scientific discovery from data.

核心方法： MorphingNet introduces a novel approach to symbolic regression by treating the network's function forms as trainable parameters, enabling continuous optimization. It uses pole-free rational functions or complex exponential-logarithm functions that can morph between various mathematical forms during training, eliminating the need for a fixed operator library.

世界不会等待：在实时时钟下评估和教授智能体

Large language model (LLM) agents can solve complex interactive tasks, yet reasoning and tool calls take time. In continuously changing environments, an otherwise correct action may arrive too late. We introduce RealtimeBench, with 180 tasks across six game types, to evaluate agents while games continue running during inference. Paired evaluations show that GPT-6.1-sol outperforms the faster Jev when games are paused, but falls behind when they keep running. We propose Two-Timescale Offline Recursive Self-Improvement (TORSI): a slow teacher studies failures and tests corrections in paused games between attempts, then writes reusable instructions for a fast student. Two rounds raise Jev's average test score from 13.51 to 24.19 on a 0--100 progress scale, improving all six game types. The instructions also improve average scores for three other models without retuning. These findings point toward real-time intelligence: enabling powerful LLM agents to remain effective in a continuously changing world by organizing deliberation, learning, and action across timescales.

From Global to Tail Convergence: Dynamics of $α$-Stable Score-Based Generative Models

从全局到尾部收敛：基于α-稳定分数驱动的生成模型动态研究

Score-based generative models driven by $α$-stable L{é}vy processes have been proposed as an alternative to Brownian diffusion models, with empirical evidence suggesting improved sampling and better modeling of heavy-tailed data. Yet theoretical guarantees for these models remain limited. We develop a non-asymptotic convergence analysis for scale-preserving $α$-stable generative dynamics, covering both compactly supported and power-law targets. In p-Wasserstein distance, we quantify the effects of early stopping, initialization, score approximation, and time discretization, and show exponential decay of the initialization error. A comparison with Brownian dynamics reveals that global Wasserstein convergence alone does not fundamentally distinguish the two models: both initialization errors decay exponentially with the forward horizon on the common class of heavy-tailed targets. A sharp separation emerges instead in the tails. At any positive generation time, stable dynamics produces power-law tails with index $α$, whereas Brownian dynamics remains light-tailed. For a target with tail index $ν$, asymptotically correct extreme-quantile scaling is possible precisely when $α$=$ν$. These results identify tail-index matching as a principled mechanism behind the ability of stable generative models to capture extreme events.

High-Probability Regret Bounds for Feel-Good Thompson Sampling

Feel-good Thompson sampling (FGTS) augments the posterior of vanilla Thompson sampling with an exploration term that favors reward functions with high predicted optimal values. This modification achieves minimax-optimal regret up to logarithmic factors in linear contextual bandits, but existing analyses of its two standard variants establish regret guarantees in expectation. In this paper, we derive the first high-probability regret bounds for both cumulative and current-step FGTS in contextual bandits with general realizable function approximation. Our bounds depend on the corresponding decoupling coefficients and the prior mass near the true reward function. In the $d$-dimensional linear setting, both variants satisfy $\mathrm{Regret}(T)=O\big(d\sqrt T\log(2T)+\sqrt T\log(1/δ)\big)$ with probability at least $1-δ$, where $T$ is the time horizon. This bound matches the dimension and horizon dependence of algorithms based on the upper confidence bounds, up to logarithmic factors. Relative to the expected-regret guarantees, the cost of controlling the regret with high probability is confined to additive terms with at most linear dependence on $\log(1/δ)$. The proof ideas are partially developed by GPT-6 Astra under the authors' guidance.

PFedMA: Who Should Join Next as Federated Multi-Agent Orchestration Evolves?

Federated Multi-Agent Systems (FedMAS) offer a promising paradigm for collaborative reasoning among LLM-based agents while keeping raw client data decentralized. However, reliable multi-round collaboration remains challenging. Agent selection must take into consideration evolving task requirements and the agent's historical reliability, while privacy rules must adapt to changing collaboration contexts and meet the client's personalized needs. To address these challenges, we propose PFedMA, a federated multi-agent framework for feedback driven multi-round collaboration under heterogeneous privacy requirements. Across rounds, PFedMA maintains historical capability and reliability states to guide agent selection, evaluates task responses before aggregation, and refines subsequent queries according to accumulated collaboration feedback. PFedMA enables bidirectional local to global and global to local private rule evolution across heterogeneous clients. Clients generalize locally updated privacy rules and selectively incorporate globally maintained rules, thereby facilitating cross-client privacy rule evolution while preserving personalized constraints. Extensive experiments across reasoning, privacy sensitive, and adversarial settings show that PFedMA improves task performance, reduces measured privacy leakage, and improves system robustness.
