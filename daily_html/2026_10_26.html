Understanding Communication Failures in Multi-Agent LLM Systems

理解多智能体大语言模型系统中的通信故障

Multi-agent LLM systems aim to improve reasoning through debate, evidence sharing, and mutual correction, but interaction can also introduce failures that final accuracy alone cannot explain. Agents may converge too early, ignore dissent, suppress correct minority answers, or preserve a wrong framing across rounds. We present a corpus-grounded taxonomy of communication failures, built from 3,211 failed multi-round trajectories across medical QA, coding, finance, multi-hop reasoning, ethics, and embodied text tasks. Using this taxonomy, we analyze failure distributions across datasets and system designs, round-level error dynamics, recovery hardness, majority transitions, minority-signal suppression, and self-reported confidence. We find that failures are dominated by recovery/correction and consensus/decision problems rather than isolated first-round mistakes, with many emerging after agents first observe peer responses. Based on these findings, we recommend stronger disagreement handling, evidence verification, minority-signal preservation, and recovery checks before finalization.

AlphaMemo: Structured Search-Process Memory for Self-Evolving Alpha Mining Agents

LLM agents are promising for alpha mining via combining financial priors, symbolic reasoning, executable factor generation, and feedback-driven refinement. To address these challenges, we propose AlphaMemo, a self-evolving alpha mining agent with Structured Search-Process Memory. We benchmark 16 deep learning architectures for time series forecasting, including Transformers, State Space Models (SSMs), xLSTMs and recurrent hybrids, on a 15-year daily futures dataset using an end-to-end volatility-targeted Sharpe ratio optimisation task. These findings suggest that success on standard time-series benchmarks does not reliably transfer to finance, and that architectures tailored to the unique properties of asset returns may remain more effective than generic attention-based approaches for financial forecasting.

Don't Forget What You Just Learned: Self-Directed Repair Turns Agent Mistakes Into Lasting Lessons

勿忘所学：自我导向修复将代理错误转化为持久教训

Intelligent agents should learn from their mistakes so that experience leads to continuous improvement. Yet today's agents recover from a mistake only temporarily: in-context reflections are easily forgotten across sessions and compete for limited context, while full-parameter fine-tuning is expensive and can interfere with existing capabilities. We introduce Self-Direction, a mechanism for agents to learn from mistakes that combines the benefits of temporary in-context retries and policy updates. When an agent fails a task, it reflects on its own trajectory in order to extract a lesson and immediately distills it into a lightweight low-rank adapter. By keeping the original weights frozen, Self-Direction prevents adapter training from overwriting base-model parameters while training additional parameters equivalent to ~0.02% of the base model's parameter count for each adapter. The agent then saves these adapters in a persistent library of lessons, automatically routing them to solve related problems later. Experiments with agents based on open-weight models from the Qwen family across open-ended research (GAIA), tool-use workflows (Claw-Eval), and terminal tasks (Terminal-Bench 2.1) show that our method improves overall success rates by 8.9 to 17.0 percentage points and performs competitively with frontier systems. We find that Self-Direction reduces repeated mistakes across future encounters of a task, nearly doubling the reliability of an agent's corrections. Learned lessons also transfer: a handful of adapters generalize to dozens of new tasks without additional training. Ultimately, this self-directed repair transforms everyday mistakes into lasting lessons.

核心方法： Self-Direction mechanism combining in-context reflections with lightweight low-rank adapters to learn from mistakes persistently without interfering with existing capabilities.
数据来源： Experiments conducted on open-ended research (GAIA), tool-use workflows (Claw-Eval), and terminal tasks (Terminal-Bench 2.1).

EVOLVE：基于自适应原型记忆的不确定性感知多元时间序列异常预测

Unsupervised multivariate time series anomaly prediction (AP) aims to estimate future anomaly risks from current observations, providing early-warning signals for proactive intervention and maintenance in complex systems. Existing unsupervised AP methods follow a perturbation-based paradigm, where predefined or random perturbations are injected into historical windows to simulate abnormal future evolution. However, these methods share perturbations across different variables and contexts, and learn deterministic history-to-future evolution. In reality, anomaly evolution is variable- and context-dependent: the same perturbation may be amplified into abnormal future dynamics in one variable or context, while being absorbed as normal fluctuation in another. In addition, future evolution is not uniquely determined by a historical window, since similar histories may lead to multiple plausible trajectories, such as gradual degradation or abrupt changes. To address these problems, we propose EVOLVE, a unified framework that models unsupervised AP via variable- and context-conditioned perturbations and uncertainty-aware future reasoning. EVOLVE constructs context-specific anomaly perturbations through an adaptive anomaly prototype memory, which can dynamically update and retrieve relevant perturbations at the variable level. EVOLVE models uncertainty by sampling multiple latent historical states and decoding through complementary smooth and deviation-amplifying evolution hypotheses, and the disagreement among decoded future trajectories is used to estimate future anomaly risk. Extensive experiments on six real-world benchmark datasets demonstrate that EVOLVE consistently outperforms state-of-the-art anomaly prediction baselines.

Conditional Flow Models for Offline Maximum Entropy Reinforcement Learning

条件流模型在离线最大熵强化学习中的应用

Offline maximum entropy reinforcement learning seeks to estimate the optimal soft action-value function \(Q^*\) from a fixed dataset without further interaction with the environment. Although \(Q^*\) is characterized by the soft optimal Bellman equation, the corresponding Bellman operator cannot be evaluated directly because both the reward distribution and the transition kernel are unknown. Consequently, estimating \(Q^*\) requires the joint treatment of environment modeling and value function learning. We propose a framework that addresses these tasks separately. Conditional diffusion models based on probability flow ODEs estimate the conditional reward distribution and transition kernel. The resulting distributional estimators define a data driven approximation of the soft optimal Bellman operator, whose empirical Bellman residual is minimized over a class of deep neural networks to estimate \(Q^*\).
