Entropy-Regularized Reinforcement Learning for the Regime-Switching Consumption-Investment Problem

基于熵正则化强化学习的机制转换消费投资问题研究

This paper establishes an entropy-regularized reinforcement learning (ERRL) framework for the continuous-time consumption-investment problem under a regime-switching market model with general utility functions. By incorporating randomized exploratory policies and Shannon entropy regularization into the stochastic optimal control formulation, we derive closed-form representations for the optimal exploratory consumption and investment strategies. Specifically, the optimal investment strategy follows a multivariate Gaussian distribution, whereas the optimal consumption strategy takes the form of a Gibbs measure---which specializes to a Gamma distribution under logarithmic utility and a non-standard exponential distribution under power utility. Crucially, our theoretical analysis reveals that the classical deterministic optimal decisions correspond to the mode---rather than the mean---of these exploratory distributions. Furthermore, we establish the convergence of the exploratory value function to its classical unregularized counterpart as the temperature parameter vanishes. For algorithmic design, we develop a continuous-time q-learning framework grounded in a novel continuous-time q-function, prove the policy improvement theorem, and establish martingale characterizations for both on-policy and off-policy learning. We find that parameterizing the actor network based on theoretical distribution families can help accelerate convergence and enhance numerical stability. Furthermore, numerical experiments across regime-switching environments provide empirical support for our theoretical findings, demonstrating stable loss convergence as well as the monotonicity and concavity of the learned value functions.

It incorporates randomized exploratory policies and Shannon entropy regularization into the stochastic optimal control formulation to derive optimal exploratory consumption and investment strategies, which are represented in closed form (multivariate Gaussian for investment, Gibbs measure for consumption). A continuous-time q-learning framework is developed for algorithmic design, with theoretical convergence guarantees.

Versatile Graph Foundation Models from Frozen Time-Series Foundation Models via Parallel Flows

通过平行流将冻结时间序列基础模型转化为多功能图基础模型

Graph foundation models aim to serve unseen graphs across tasks, feature spaces, and label sets. Most current models learn how to represent a new graph by pre-training on other graphs. Because graph data are scarce and differ across domains in features and structures, what they learn may not transfer to a graph from an unseen domain. We show that a frozen time-series foundation model, pre-trained on abundant time series, can instead serve as a graph foundation model without graph pre-training and without any gradient update on the target graph. Such a model reads ordered multivariate sequences, but a graph has no time axis, and covering all its edges with sequences is nontrivial because a node can have many neighbors. We therefore propose GraphFlowFM, which decomposes a graph into parallel flows, sets of vertex-disjoint paths that jointly cover every edge, and reads each path as a multivariate time series with the nodes as time steps. A frozen TimesFM encodes the paths with causal attention, which gives each node a self token and a context token, and a closed-form ridge readout weighs these tokens by their alignment with the labels. In node classification, GraphFlowFM attains the best average rank on 21 datasets among graph foundation models published in 2025 and 2026, ranks first on 17 datasets, and leads on six of the seven heterophilous datasets. It also exceeds pre-trained graph foundation models in link prediction on all six datasets and ranks second in molecule classification, behind only a model fine-tuned after pre-training on molecules.

核心方法： GraphFlowFM utilizes a frozen time-series foundation model to process graphs by decomposing them into parallel vertex-disjoint paths treated as multivariate time series. The model employs causal attention to encode these paths and a closed-form ridge readout to weigh tokens based on label alignment.
数据来源： The model can be applied to any graph-structured financial data such as transaction networks, payment networks, or asset correlation graphs. The time-series foundation model is pre-trained on abundant generic time-series data.
Alpha 潜力：

This approach can uncover complex relational patterns in financial networks (e.g., inter-asset relationships, order book dynamics) that traditional time-series models might miss. The ability to handle heterophilous graphs (where connected nodes may differ) is particularly valuable for detecting anomalous relationships or new market regimes.

Decomposing Irregular Time Series into Interpretable Temporal Patterns with Mixture-of-Experts

基于专家混合模型的可解释时间模式分解不规则时间序列

Irregularly-spaced sequential data, such as transaction logs, encode multi-scale temporal structure that is critical for prediction but difficult to recover explicitly with standard sequence models, which often trade interpretability for performance. We introduce MoTIF, an interpretable framework using mixture-of-experts (MoE) attention to decompose irregular time series into temporal patterns spanning minute-to-year scales. Each expert is trained via temporal alignment that induces attention specialization to a designated cyclical or within-range pattern, with label-free routing supervision that learns to activate the correct expert from patterns present in the data. On synthetic data with known temporal structure, MoTIF recovers correct routing well above chance (0.389/0.829 for range/cyclical patterns vs. 0.091 random) and outperforms all baselines, including a standard transformer and an unspecialized MoE, on next-value prediction. On a public credit card transaction dataset, MoTIF achieves higher macro-F1 than all baselines for next-amount prediction. Its learned routing reveals structure a black-box model cannot: transactions are governed primarily by intraday and short-interval rhythms, while fraudulent transactions show bursty, calendar-agnostic timing versus the steadier periodicity of legitimate ones. On downstream fraud detection, MoTIF matches standard transformer and MoE baselines and clearly outperforms recent sequential recommendation models used as embedding baselines. These results show that decomposing temporal structure into interpretable, expert-specific patterns need not sacrifice predictive accuracy, offering a practical route to sequence models that are both accurate and auditable in high-stakes settings. Code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/MoTIF-82E4.

数据来源： The paper evaluates MoTIF on synthetic data with known temporal structure and a public credit card transaction dataset, which includes legitimate and fraudulent transactions.

Pricing Today, Shaping Tomorrow: Contextual Dynamic Pricing in Purchase-Driven Evolving Systems

今日定价，塑造未来：采购驱动演变系统中的情境动态定价

Across diverse markets, it is important for firms to balance attracting customers with managing operational conditions, such as workloads and delivery times. Dynamic pricing plays a central role in this tradeoff, as the orders generated today can change the conditions faced by future customers. We develop a novel framework for contextual dynamic pricing with an observed shared state and unknown purchase-driven transitions. The valuation model allows unknown mean functions of customer covariates and state, unknown state-specific scales and a common unknown standardized noise distribution. We propose Shadow-Adjusted Pricing in Evolving Systems (SHAPE), which incorporates a purchase's future revenue consequences into current pricing. Purchase-shadow adjustment and scale standardization reveal a common one-dimensional oracle price map across states, allowing SHAPE to retain its pricing history across value updates without repeating coarse exploration. The same active sales records support price-map learning and joint updates of continuation values and purchase shadows, without fitting the noise distribution or full transition matrices. To support this reuse under purchase-driven feedback, we establish pricing guarantees for history-dependent inputs and stability bounds for the joint updates. Under regularity, coverage and design conditions, we obtain sublinear expected regret against a finite-horizon dynamic oracle for linear, Hölder and sparse linear mean models. For affine valuations at fixed state and feature dimensions, SHAPE matches the static semiparametric minimax time exponent up to logarithmic factors for every fixed noise-tail smoothness order $β\ge2$. Numerical experiments further demonstrate the appealing performance of SHAPE.

核心方法： The paper introduces SHAPE (Shadow-Adjusted Pricing in Evolving Systems), a contextual dynamic pricing framework that balances customer attraction with operational conditions. Optimal portfolio and consumption under no-shorting constraints: The exploratory formulation and reinforcement learning

无卖空约束下的最优投资组合与消费：探索性建模与强化学习

We develop a reinforcement-learning framework for the continuous-time optimal portfolio-consumption via utility maximization under no-shorting portfolio constraints. We adopt the exploratory formulation under entropy regularization and impose no-shorting constraint through a componentwise nonnegative conditional-mean constraint on the class of admissible randomized policies, while the sampled actions are allowed to be negative during the training. For CRRA utility, we derive the homothetic value function and the optimal Gaussian portfolio and Gibbs consumption policies under some model conditions and establish a verification theorem. These results allow compatible parameterizations in actor, critic, and $q$ functions for a constrained policy-iteration algorithm. Policy evaluation uses trajectory-based moment conditions, and policy improvement uses fitted coefficients to solve a convex quadratic program over the nonnegative orthant without requiring market coefficients as inputs. The mean constraint preserves tractable Gaussian exploration throughout parameter learning, while modal deployment converts the learned randomized policies into deterministic controls. In particular, the Gaussian mode coincides with its constrained mean and hence satisfies no-shorting constraint, and the Gibbs mode determines the deployed consumption rule.

Non-asymptotic analysis of SGLD with discontinuous stochastic gradients: a new route to portfolio CVaR minimization

SGLD非渐进分析与间断随机梯度：投资组合CVaR最小化的新途径

Conditional-Value-at-Risk (CVaR) optimization is a fundamental tool in portfolio selection, yet its nonsmooth loss structure poses a challenge for the existing theory of stochastic gradient methods including associated Langevin algorithms. In particular, the positive-part loss and the softmax portfolio parametrization lead to stochastic gradients that need not be globally Lipschitz continuous, but rather discontinuous, where available non-convex stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD) guarantees often exhibit an unfavorable exponential dependence on the problem dimension. In this article, we develop a non-asymptotic analysis of SGLD for a regularized CVaR portfolio optimization problem. We exploit the geometric structure of the CVaR objective to establish linear growth, convexity-at-infinity, and one-sided Lipschitz properties of the stochastic gradient without imposing density assumptions on the asset losses that are typical in previous works. Crucially, the constants appearing in the aforementioned structural conditions are shown to be independent of the number of assets. Moreover, we establish a convergence bound in Wasserstein-2 distance with rate $\mathcal{O}(λ^{1/4})$ and a finite-time guarantee for the expected excess risk. Our analysis, together with the dimension-independent constants in the structural conditions, leads to an excess risk bound that depends linearly on the number of assets. Numerical experiments with simulated Gaussian portfolios exhibit the predicted dimension dependence. Computational comparisons with alternative stochastic optimization methods further illustrate the practical efficiency of SGLD for the optimization problem under consideration. Finally, we extend the framework to a broader class of non-convex stochastic optimization problems with discontinuous stochastic gradients.

核心方法： The paper proposes a non-asymptotic analysis of Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) for regularized CVaR portfolio optimization, addressing the challenge of discontinuous stochastic gradients in nonsmooth loss structures. It leverages the geometric properties of the CVaR objective to establish dimension-independent convergence guarantees.

Non-Convex Market Makers: Coherence without Convexity, Friction, and Parallel Composition

非凸市场做市商：无凸性、摩擦和平行组合的连贯性

Cost-function market makers are always assumed convex, and convexity is not what makes them coherent. For a path-independent maker the exact no-arbitrage condition is a chord condition, that every chord slope of the cost lie in the convex hull of payoffs; convexity enters the standard axiomatics through a separate requirement, monotone information incorporation, and dropping it leaves coherent non-convex makers. Rational flow against such a maker trades the convex envelope, so non-convexity costs expressiveness rather than soundness, and a proportional fee of at least the arbitrage depth restores no-arbitrage, which by conjugation is a bid-ask spread with an exact no-trade interval for any cost whatever. Combining fee-bearing makers is an infimal convolution solved by one monotone clearing-price root-find, with sparse participation and a consolidated limit order book as the aggregate, and a deep quadratic co-quoter is Moreau smoothing, which preserves coherence at every depth. Any quoted configuration outside the coherent hull admits a portfolio priced below its worst payoff, so arbitrageurs act as decentralized separation oracles, and coherence is relative to friction and to the cost of finding certificates.

RaMMOBO: Random Max-Margin Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization

RaMMOBO: 随机最大边距多目标贝叶斯优化

Multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) maximizes $M$ expensive black-box objectives with the goal of approximating the Pareto set, whose objective vectors cannot be improved in one component without sacrificing another. A common quality measure is hypervolume, the volume in objective space dominated by these vectors relative to a reference point. Expected hypervolume improvement (EHVI) directly targets this metric and is one-step Bayes-optimal, but its standard computation scales exponentially with $M$, making it prohibitively expensive for many-objective problems. We introduce random max-margin multi-objective Bayesian optimization (RaMMOBO), whose acquisition function lower-bounds EHVI while requiring only linear cost in $M$ to evaluate. RaMMOBO measures the volume of a max-margin box contained in a candidate's hypervolume-improvement region and averages over random reweightings of the objective space, yielding a utility that is strictly Pareto-monotone on hypervolume-improving outcomes. It naturally handles noisy observations and different probabilistic surrogates and supports efficient gradient-based optimization. Across synthetic and real-world benchmarks, RaMMOBO matches or outperforms state-of-the-art methods, with largest gains at higher $M$, with acquisition optimization up to two orders of magnitude faster than qLogNEHVI. Our implementation is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/rammobo.

核心方法： Introduces a novel multi-objective Bayesian optimization method (RaMMOBO) that efficiently approximates the Pareto set by lower-bounding expected hypervolume improvement (EHVI) with linear computational cost in the number of objectives.
数据来源： Generic black-box objectives; demonstrated on synthetic and real-world benchmarks (specific finance datasets not mentioned but methodology is transferable).

Robust Reward Machine Learning and Execution in Noisy Environments

噪声环境中的鲁棒性奖励机器学习与执行

Background: Reward machines (RMs) represent non-Markovian reward functions as finite-state machines over high-level propositional events. Traditionally, these events are generated by a deterministic labelling function that maps environment states to proposition sets. However, real-world labelling functions are inherently noisy. Objectives: Design and implement a method that learns RMs in the presence of noisy labelling functions and exploits them for policy learning in both discrete and continuous environments. Methods: We propose Prob-IRM, a method that learns RMs from noisy proposition histories using inductive logic programming (ILP) and exploits them for policy learning. Noisy proposition histories are represented as training examples for ILP, which learns an RM that maximises coverage of low-uncertainty histories. RM learning is interleaved with policy optimisation. Prob-IRM uses a belief over RM states to determine when to relearn the RM and to provide additional guiding rewards. Results: Prob-IRM is evaluated on environments with discrete and continuous state spaces under varying levels of labelling noise. Prob-IRM performs comparably to baselines using handcrafted RMs and outperforms approaches that use recurrent neural networks to encode history, despite operating with noisy labels. In this paper, we formulate clustering analysis as discrete generative modeling. Extensive experiments on several benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and desirable properties of DGC. The code is publicly available at GitHub.
