From Alpha to Execution: Trading Horizons, Regimes, and Schedules under Concave Impact and Hurst Scaling

从Alpha到执行：考虑凹性冲击和Hurst标度下的交易时间窗口、市场状态与执行调度

Classical execution models assume linear impact, diffusive prices, and an exogenous horizon. We study execution under concave power-law impact and Hurst-scaled price fluctuations, and make two contributions. First, we choose the execution horizon by maximizing the Sharpe ratio of the trade's realized P&L. Price moves during the execution window are part of that P&L, so executing more slowly reduces its variance, and because impact is concave it can also lower cost per share. Urgency comes only from alpha realized while the order is being worked. The problem is governed by the signs of two quantities: (i) the duration elasticity of impact $a$, which under a structural prior equals the impact-persistence gap $β- H$ and can take either sign; and (ii) the net urgency $ν$, the rate of alpha realization net of exposure relief. When $ν$ keeps its sign over the window, an interior optimum exists, to leading order in $T/t_f$, only when both are positive; otherwise the order should be worked at the fastest or slowest admissible pace. In the interior regime the first-order condition involves no impact coefficient, order size or volume, and the objective is flat: for a large-cap example, the window within 1% of maximal Sharpe spans more than an order of magnitude. Second, given the window, we derive the schedule shape by convex programming under an envelope cost. For any increasing per-share cost of delay $w(t)$, the KKT solution is $φ\propto (λ- w(t))_+^{1/β}$. For a Hurst-scaled envelope $w \propto t^H$, the normalized schedule depends only on $(β, H)$, has a closed form via the incomplete beta function, is front-loaded, and tapers smoothly to zero, saving 10-17% of envelope cost relative to a constant-rate (TWAP) schedule of equal duration. Whether it uses the whole window depends on a single dimensionless robustness level, for which we give a calibration.

核心方法： The paper presents a novel approach to optimal execution by considering concave power-law impact and Hurst-scaled price fluctuations. It introduces two key contributions: (1) determining the execution horizon by maximizing the Sharpe ratio of the realized P&L, considering both impact concavity and alpha realization, and (2) deriving optimal execution schedules using convex programming under an envelope cost.

Cross-product Modelling in Continuous Intraday Electricity Markets via Hawkes Processes

基于霍克斯过程的连续日内电力市场跨产品建模

As renewable energy integration accelerates, intraday continuous (IDC) electricity markets have become essential for short-term balancing. However, the simultaneous trading of overlapping contracts with varying granularities creates complex, hidden liquidity dependencies that are critical to market stability but remain poorly understood. This paper proposes a multivariate State-Dependent Hawkes Process (SDHP) to model cross-product liquidity dynamics between Quarter-Hourly and Hourly IDC contracts. To determine how cross-product effects vary under different market conditions, we classify the trading sessions into two distinct regimes: 'Normal' and 'Stressed', based on a composite metric of the bid-ask spread, mid-price volatility and queue imbalance. The model is calibrated on the first month of each quarter of 2024 using level 3 limit order book (LOB) data from the Dutch EPEX SPOT IDC market. We find that order flow is overwhelmingly self-driven, with roughly 95\% of orders arriving in reaction to earlier orders in both regimes. Cross-excitation runs mainly from the Quarter-Hourly to the Hourly contract, but this leadership weakens under stress: the ratio of cumulative cross-excitation falls from 2.8 to 1.5, so the two contracts influence each other almost equally. Stress also makes the Quarter-Hourly to Hourly channel much faster, cutting its mean lag from 6.05 to 0.93 seconds. The strong seasonal variation found in calm periods largely disappears under stress, where all four months converge to the same structure.

自动化市场中的智能体AI

We study how heterogeneous agent behavior shapes stress, risk-adjusted performance, liquidity, and volatility in these types of markets. Three behavioral archetypes are modeled as agentic reinforcement learning (RL) systems: a rational arbitrageur, a manipulator, and a retail herd-follower. Each archetype is defined by its reward function within a common, asset-agnostic exposure-control environment, and each is trained with a value-based Deep Q-Network (DQN) and with REINFORCE using a learned baseline. The results motivate behavior-specific regulation rather than uniform limits on volatility or turnover.

LagNet: Lag-Structured Operators for Long-Term Time Series Forecasting

LagNet: 基于滞后结构算子的长期时间序列预测

Long-term time series forecasting maps a finite history window to an extended future horizon. Many existing models focus primarily on the design of input representations and model structures, while paying less attention to the structural form of the history-to-future forecasting operator itself. We examine this operator directly and find that, across model families, sensitivity matrices exhibit recurring diagonal patterns whose diagonals correspond to relative temporal lags. Motivated by this observation, we develop a framework for direct multi-step forecasting based on lag-structured temporal operators. Its basic building block, LagLayer, constructs a Toeplitz matrix by tying history-to-future connections with the same relative lag and incorporates horizon-wise and history-wise scaling coefficients to relax exact sharing. This design enables LagLayer to serve as an effective replacement for temporal linear layers in existing forecasting models. Stacking LagLayer modules with activations and multiple feature maps yields LagNet. Experiments on long-term time series forecasting benchmarks show that our method is competitive with strong baselines, while sensitivity matrix diagnostics and limited-data experiments demonstrate that LagLayer provides an effective and sample-efficient alternative to a linear layer.

Combinatorial Arbitrage in Prediction Markets

预测市场中的组合套利

Our results establish a promising path from manually designed algorithms toward general algorithm models that learn, generalize, and evolve from computational experience.

Flat Loss, Evolving Features: Grokking in Modular Addition

Can a network keep learning while its training cross-entropy stays nearly flat at a positive level? We show that it can. On modular addition, a standard testbed for grokking, ReLU networks trained with supported-Polar hidden updates, normalized output-layer updates and weight decay can keep 100% training accuracy while their hidden features change and held-out accuracy rises from zero to above 90%, at relatively flat training cross-entropy. Correct classification need not minimize cross-entropy, which still depends on prediction confidence. We prove this coexistence for one-hidden-layer networks in two finite-window theorems from constructed initializations: one gives a constant gain in the Fourier fraction of the hidden features, and the other holds at arbitrarily low positive loss, where a readout refitted on the training data generalizes before the network. Experiments from random initialization show the same coexistence on a window fixed in advance, and in deeper networks held-out accuracy also rises over post-hoc windows with a net increase in training cross-entropy. With a last-layer rate set per training fraction and head decay scaled with the prime, deeper networks at p=47 and 61 meet a criterion on post-hoc windows, fixed in advance, in nearly all fresh runs. A one-block transformer trained with the same updates and a slowly decaying output gain keeps every training pair correct at every logged checkpoint while its training cross-entropy rises and its held-out accuracy approaches one; with two blocks this holds through the held-out rise, after which training pairs begin to flip.
