LENS: Reliability-Aware Stock Prediction via LLM-Extracted Priors and News-Driven Semantic Graphs

LENS：基于LLM提取先验和新闻驱动语义图的可靠性感知股票预测

Predicting stock returns requires modeling persistent company relations and time-varying market signals. Graph Neural Network (GNN) approaches combine static priors with dynamic relational graphs, but treat every extracted edge as a trustworthy binary fact, ignoring heterogeneous reliability across relational sources. We propose LENS (LLM-Extracted priors and News-driven Semantics), a static--dynamic graph framework for stock return prediction and ranking that models relational reliability as a first-class signal across the pipeline. On the static side, we extract a multi-relational company graph from a Large Language Model and aggregate it over multiple runs with confidence weighting to downweight low-consistency edges. On the dynamic side, we build a news-driven semantic graph with exponential-decay temporal aggregation to capture changing market context under uneven coverage. A context-dependent gated fusion module rebalances the two graphs at the per-stock, per-day level. On S&P 500, LENS improves AR@5 by 16.4% and SR@5 by 22.6% over the strongest baseline, and on NASDAQ 100 it achieves comparable improvements (15.5% and 20.9% respectively). Ablations isolate each component's contribution, and a human-validation study of sampled relations supports that the aggregation is grounded, providing evidence that reliability-aware LLM relations and news-based semantic graphs carry complementary information for graph-based financial prediction.

核心方法： LENS combines static multi-relational company graphs extracted from LLMs with dynamic news-driven semantic graphs, using confidence weighting for static edges and exponential-decay temporal aggregation for dynamic graphs. A context-dependent gated fusion module balances the two graphs.
数据来源： Uses Large Language Model (LLM) extracted company relations and news data for building semantic graphs, applied to S&P 500 and NASDAQ 100 stocks.

Koopman Generative Operators for Efficient Probabilistic Time-Series Forecasting

用于高效概率时间序列预测的Koopman生成算子

Probabilistic time-series forecasting requires models that simultaneously capture structured temporal dynamics, expressive uncertainty, and efficient inference, yet existing approaches fall short of this goal: latent dynamical models impose structure but rely on restrictive generation mechanisms, while modern generative methods such as diffusion and flow matching achieve flexibility at the cost of iterative and computationally expensive sampling. We introduce the Koopman Generative Operator (KGO), a probabilistic forecasting method that conceptualizes prediction as the evolution of structured uncertainty. KGO integrates three core components: (1) Koopman Patch Embedding (KoPE) for temporally consistent latent trajectory extrapolation; (2) Koopman Flow Matching (KoFM), which enables fast, single-step generation through a closed-form matrix exponential in a Koopman latent space, bypassing iterative sampling; and (3) an Adaptive Uncertainty Gate (AUG), which provides probabilistic forecasts by adapting uncertainty per-variable and per-horizon, corresponding to a learned decomposition of aleatoric uncertainty without manual tuning. By unifying these components, KGO achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on 13/17 ProbTS benchmarks in CRPS and 16/17 in NMAE. Further, by eliminating iterative sampling, KGO delivers at least a 25$\times$ reduction in inference time compared to iterative generative models. These results position KGO as a practical and principled framework that advances the accuracy-efficiency trade-off in probabilistic time-series forecasting, combining leading accuracy on the evaluated benchmarks with fast, single-step generation.

核心方法： Koopman Generative Operator (KGO) combines Koopman Patch Embedding (KoPE) for latent trajectory extrapolation, Koopman Flow Matching (KoFM) for fast single-step generation, and Adaptive Uncertainty Gate (AUG) for probabilistic forecasting. This approach integrates structured temporal dynamics with efficient uncertainty modeling.

CATF: A Manager-Worker Framework for Context-Aware Time-Series Forecasting

CATF：基于管理者-工作者框架的上下文感知时间序列预测

We study context-aware time-series forecasting, in which a single target variable is predicted from its own history and a set of contextual variables, such as weather and calendar information. As the context becomes more diverse, a single model struggles to capture all of its regimes, while ensemble learning and Mixture-of-Experts approaches rely on manual data partitioning or soft routing, which increases the inference cost and hinders interpretability. We propose CATF, a Context-Aware Time-Series Forecasting framework built on a Manager-Worker architecture: a Transformer-based Manager routes each input to a single Worker based on the context, using hard routing during both training and inference. The Workers thus specialize without manual data partitioning, and any single-model forecaster can serve as a Worker. On five context-rich datasets, four horizons, and two normalization schemes, CATF improves over its standalone counterpart in 162 of 200 comparisons, the best CATF-enhanced model outperforms the best standalone baseline in 19 of 20 settings, and it outperforms the zero-shot Time-MoE foundation model in 15 of 20 settings. A case study on bike-sharing data shows that the Manager routes inputs by the calendar and that each Worker specializes in a different weekly pattern.

核心方法： Manager-Worker architecture with Transformer-based Manager for hard routing of inputs to specialized Workers based on context, enabling context-aware time-series forecasting

We ask how much of that predictability survives a test that rules out look-ahead, memorization and selection. We propose OLIVIA, a protocol for such tests: point-in-time inputs with company identities masked, selection on validation data only, an analysis plan frozen by cryptographic hash, and a test period unblinded once through a logged lock. We apply it to AI decision models, non-generative language models that return probabilities for typed questions, reading news about S&P 500 stocks: an open-weights model we fine-tuned on 2024-2025 data, three commercial models and standard baselines. The fine-tuned model's signal replicated on two validation quarters (daily information coefficients 0.055 and 0.065) but fell to 0.020 (t = 1.7) on the blinded January-July 2026 test.
