CorrFlow: Exact and Permutation-Equivariant Flow Matching on the Correlation Elliptope

CorrFlow：相关性椭球上的精确且置换等变的流匹配

Correlation matrices combine nonlinear positive-definiteness constraints with a simultaneous row-column permutation symmetry. Their generation therefore involves two distinct problems. First, every output must remain on the correlation elliptope, irrespective of how its variables are ordered. Second, the generator must learn the domain-specific distribution occupied by empirical data inside that valid space. Existing financial image generators mix these problems: they learn ordered matrix entries and repair invalid draws afterward. We introduce CorrFlow, which separates them. A generalized Fisher transform maps full-rank correlation matrices bijectively to unconstrained coordinates, guaranteeing valid outputs without projection. A graph flow-matching network learns the empirical distribution in those coordinates and is permutation equivariant: reordering the variables only reorders the output. We compare a conditional CorrGAN reimplementation, image diffusion, and CorrFlow on $80\times80$ Taiwan equity matrices using five seeds per neural method. CorrFlow produces 100% valid draws without repair and obtains feature sliced-Wasserstein distance $.712\pm.141$, versus $4.339\pm.252$ for CorrGAN and $4.610\pm.191$ for image diffusion after their invalid outputs are projected back to the correlation space. It also improves this distance over historical lookup, while lookup retains higher local coverage. These results support the combined construction under the evaluated training budget; they do not establish superiority over fully tuned image models or isolate the contribution of each component.

核心方法： CorrFlow introduces a novel approach to generating valid correlation matrices by separating the problems of maintaining positive-definiteness and permutation symmetry from learning the empirical distribution. It uses a generalized Fisher transform for bijective mapping to unconstrained coordinates and a graph flow-matching network that is permutation equivariant.
数据来源： The paper evaluates the method on $80\times80$ Taiwan equity matrices, indicating application to financial market correlation data.
Alpha 潜力：

The methodology could significantly enhance portfolio optimization and risk management strategies by generating more accurate and valid correlation matrices, which are crucial for covariance matrix estimation in quantitative finance.

Gaussian process solvers for linear partial differential equations (PDEs) return posteriors rather than point estimates, but they condition only on equality information: the PDE residual, boundary data, and measurements. Many solutions of interest also obey inequality constraints, including nonnegativity, monotonicity, and absence of arbitrage, which equality conditioning cannot enforce. We prove that conditioning a Gaussian process prior jointly on linear equality observations and a finite-dimensional inequality event is equivalent to conditioning sequentially in terms of Borel measures on the solution space. The physics-informed posterior is therefore a valid prior for the inequality update at virtual points. The combined law is non-Gaussian yet computable through truncated-Gaussian moments, and neither the PDE conditioning nor the inequality update requires modification. We evaluated the construction on a Poisson benchmark, option-price surfaces, and air-quality fields. Under incomplete boundary data, a monotonicity constraint lowered the error of a two-dimensional Poisson reconstruction by $63\%$. On a real S&P 500 option chain thinned to $20$ retained quotes, no-arbitrage constraints reduced held-out extrapolation error by $22\%$ and cut the calendar-arbitrage rate from $10\%$ to $1\%$. On four real air-quality snapshots, nonnegativity reduced a Gaussian moment-based sub-zero-tail proxy by $74\%$ at the sparsest monitor count. Changes in mean accuracy and constraint diagnostics differed across data regimes. The probability of the noisy virtual constraint event under the equality posterior measures agreement with the specified bounds before the update. The construction turns admissibility from a post-processing step into conditioning information for probabilistic PDE solvers.

Our findings suggest that current benchmarks for covariate-informed TSFMs can distort model comparisons by imposing fixed encoding strategies.

Feature selection is a central problem in high-dimensional financial regression, where large numbers of engineered variables and strong correlations among predictors reduce model stability and interpretability. $L_1$ regularization (Lasso) is widely used for sparse regression, but its shrinkage can be restrictive when informative signals are spread across correlated variables. This paper presents a feature selection framework based on Monte Carlo Tree Search with Generalized Nested Rollout Policy Adaptation (GNRPA). Feature selection is formulated as a sequential decision process, and candidate subsets are scored by their out-of-sample validation performance rather than training error. We study this approach on a contemporaneous explanatory regression task: explaining the weekly return of the NASDAQ-100 index from 1,072 engineered features spanning market, macroeconomic, and factor data. This isolates the value of subset selection under multicollinearity from the separate problem of predictive forecasting. On this task, the proposed method identifies sparse feature subsets with higher out-of-sample explanatory power than an optimized Lasso baseline, reaching an out-of-sample $R^2$ of 0.6961 versus 0.6313 for Lasso. These results suggest that Monte Carlo Tree Search is a practical alternative to convex regularization for sparse explanatory feature selection in financial regression, with potential value for factor attribution and risk decomposition where transparency matters as much as out-of-sample fit.

In the unconstrained setting, entropy regularization can be introduced without changing the basic quadratic structure of the dynamic program. Numerical and empirical results illustrate both the structural advantage of the state-dependent temperature and the practical relevance of the convexity-preserving temperature restriction.

Hindsight Structured Advice Distillation: Steering Executor Agents with Advisors Trained on Their Failures and Task Characteristics

后见之明结构化建议蒸馏：通过针对执行代理的失败和任务特性训练的顾问模型来引导其行为

Language-model agents are used for specialized work such as financial research, healthcare and law. Such agents are often capable enough for a task but still miss its details. In finance, for example, these details include which filing reports a figure or how a company discloses a metric. Retraining the agent to learn these details is expensive, and often impossible when the agent is a closed or shared system. Writing the missing know-how into the agent's prompt makes the prompt long and identical for every input, although different inputs need different guidance. We propose to learn to generate this guidance instead. A small advisor model reads each input and writes structured advice, which is appended to the prompt of a frozen executor. The advisor is trained with hindsight, following work on distillation with privileged information. On a training pool with graded answers, we analyse the executor's failures, run it under several strategies to see which one worked on each input, and let a teacher that sees the grades and the traces write the advice that would have helped. The advisor then learns, by supervised fine-tuning, to write that advice from the input alone. Because the student is a separate small model rather than the executor, it is cheap to train and adds little latency. We call this approach hindsight structured advice distillation (HSAD). On FrontierFinance, a public benchmark of expert-written financial research queries, an 8B advisor raises the qualification rate of Samaya's proprietary research agent, which already leads the benchmark's leaderboard, from 52.9% to 56.6%. This is above the agent's own high-effort configuration, at 37% lower cost per query. We study the generalization of the proposed approach, and in particular show that HSAD also improves results on WebShop, BIRD text-to-SQL and ExpertLongBench.

核心方法： Hindsight Structured Advice Distillation (HSAD): A method where a small advisor model generates structured advice based on input analysis and past failures of a larger frozen executor model. The advisor is trained using supervised fine-tuning on hindsight data from executor failures and successful strategies.
