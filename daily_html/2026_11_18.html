DDSL: Iterative Self-Labeling for Financial Market States

DDSL：金融市场状态迭代自标记框架

Financial market states lack a unique ground truth and must therefore be constructed from price data. Conventional labeling methods typically define states independently of the downstream classifier, leaving unresolved whether the resulting labels can be recovered from information available at prediction time. We propose DDSL, an iterative self-labeling framework that couples a price- and grammar-guided Viterbi labeler with a Transformer classifier through model feedback. DDSL constructs a nine-state representation that jointly encodes current and future market trends while retaining structured price emissions and temporal constraints throughout refinement. Experiments on SSE50, CSI300, CSI500, and NASDAQ100 show that DDSL increases held-out joint-state macro F1 by 78%-96% over single-pass dynamic-programming labeling. An independent GRU classifier that never participates in the labeling loop reproduces the same trend, while label entropy and class coverage remain stable, indicating that the gain transfers across architectures and is not caused by label collapse. We further show that learnability is controlled by both iteration and the model-feedback weight $\beta_{\mathrm{model}}$. Iterative gains diminish as successive label systems converge, whereas stronger feedback increasingly widens the performance gap between the feedback Transformer and the independent GRU. Increased learnability is accompanied by declining segment-level price-feature separation after an initial improvement. Finally, a non-anticipative event study provides partial external validation: refinement strengthens a conditional BEAR$\rightarrow$BULL reversal signal on the three A-share datasets, while the corresponding effect has the opposite sign on NASDAQ100. These results position DDSL as a structured framework for constructing market-state labels whose learnability can be explicitly controlled and evaluated.

核心方法： DDSL is an iterative self-labeling framework that combines a price- and grammar-guided Viterbi labeler with a Transformer classifier through model feedback. It constructs a nine-state representation encoding current and future market trends while maintaining structured price emissions and temporal constraints.
数据来源： Experiments conducted on SSE50, CSI300, CSI500 (Chinese A-share indices) and NASDAQ100 (US tech index).

Can Language Models Learn to Forecast Stock Prices?

语言模型能否学会预测股票价格？

We study whether language models can learn to forecast stock returns through training. We build BETA, a chronological sandbox over S&P 500 stocks in which a model calls tools to retrieve prices, indicators, and news available up to a decision date, and then forecasts the stock's future return. Evaluating frontier models in this sandbox reveals three weaknesses: their scores decline as the test period approaches the present while a simple momentum baseline stays stable, they predict gains far more often than gains occur, and they predict moves much smaller than those that are realized. We first fine-tune on filtered, hindsight-generated tool-use trajectories for historical tasks judged predictable from the evidence available at the time. We then apply reinforcement learning with rewards based on realized returns. During RL, long investigations frequently exceed the context window. We therefore introduce a selective compaction tool that allows the model to compress chosen portions of its interaction history. Selective compaction changes the context available to subsequent decisions, so policy updates must preserve the context in which each action was generated. Meanwhile, rollouts vary substantially in the number and length of their trainable turns, making a shared group-level baseline less effective. We address these challenges with Context-Aligned Turn-Level PPO, which uses a turn-level critic and computes policy updates using each turn's actual context. The resulting models, which we call AURA, substantially reduce all three weaknesses. Training improves the 8B model's score from 24.0 to 47.0 and the 30B model's from 27.4 to 54.7, with the latter surpassing all evaluated frontier models on our benchmark and achieving its largest margin on the most recent test period. These results suggest that agentic RL can improve performance on tasks with noisy, delayed real-world outcomes.

核心方法： The paper introduces BETA, a chronological sandbox for S&P 500 stocks where language models are trained to forecast stock returns using a combination of fine-tuning on filtered, hindsight-generated tool-use trajectories and reinforcement learning (RL) with rewards based on realized returns. The authors also introduce selective compaction to manage context window limitations and Context-Aligned Turn-Level PPO to handle varying rollout lengths.
数据来源： The study uses S&P 500 stock data, including prices, technical indicators, and news available up to the decision date.

Adaptive Memory for Online Reinforcement Learning under Sparse Model Changes

稀疏模型变化下的在线强化学习自适应记忆方法

We study the discounted reinforcement learning (RL) problem in a setting where the underlying environment changes between deployments. Each deployment provides $H$ observations from a fixed MDP. The reward function and transition kernel are fixed within each deployment. Across deployments, at most an $\epsilon >0$ fraction of the reward and transition kernel entries may change. In this setting, learning each model to deploy from scratch may discard useful observations from past deployments, whereas pooling all past observations can bias the estimates of entries that have already changed. We then propose an algorithm that for each state and action pair, selects how much history to reuse to estimate the reward and probabilities of the next state. We start with observations from the current deployment and add earlier deployment observations one at a time, the most recent first. After each addition, we check whether a single average reward agrees with the estimates from all histories considered until that point. We apply the same check to the distribution of next states. For each estimate, we leverage the last history that passes these aforementioned checks. The optimal control policy is then computed on top of the fitted MDP. We characterize the average gap between the optimal return and the return of the learner's policies over a finite number of deployments. We demonstrate that when $0< |S|\epsilon \leq 2$, with $|S|$ being the cardinality of the state space, our algorithm needs only the observations from the most recent $O(1/\epsilon)$ deployments, and achieves a long-run average gap of at most $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{\epsilon/H})$.

核心方法： A reinforcement learning algorithm that adaptively reuses historical data from past deployments while detecting changes in the underlying MDP (Markov Decision Process). The algorithm checks for consistency between current and historical estimates of rewards and transition probabilities, then optimally selects the amount of history to reuse for each state-action pair.
数据来源： Not explicitly financial data, but methodology is applicable to trading environments that change periodically (e.g., regime shifts, changing market conditions). Could be applied to price series, order book data, or other market microstructure data.
Alpha 潜力：

Potential to adapt to changing market regimes more efficiently than full retraining or complete history pooling. Could help strategies maintain performance during structural breaks by detecting which historical patterns remain valid. Particularly relevant for trading strategies sensitive to joint distributions (e.g., pairs trading, stat arb strategies).

The Only Edge Was Memorized in Training: LLM Trading Agents Around Earnings and Bad News

训练中唯一记住的是边缘：围绕财报和坏消息的LLM交易智能体

Large language model (LLM) trading agents are tested on news, but news reaches stock prices within minutes, and a benchmark that replays headlines at their publication time finds no alpha in trading on them. Money can then remain in two places, before news whose date is known, such as earnings announcements, and after bad news, if the market's fall went too far. However, studies of LLMs and earnings read documents published with or after the announcement, and studies of reversal sort monthly portfolios, so neither tells whether an agent can trade these moments after costs. In this paper, we test LLM agents at both moments on the same look-ahead-free benchmark, before 598 earnings announcements and after 409 bad-news drops of large US stocks. Every input is cut at the decision close, staged agents are compared with single calls, simple rules and a logistic regression, and every decision is made with and without the stock's name. The only gains came from what the models had memorized in training. Given only the ticker and the dates, a GPT-based model called the direction of 59.2% of the announcements before its training cutoff and 53.3% after it. Agent builders should therefore compare an event agent with the simple bet and test it blind after the training cutoff.

Numerical experiments on scientific discovery and image generation tasks demonstrate that our framework can effectively optimize black-box rewards in low-data scenarios.
