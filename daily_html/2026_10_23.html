Governed Self-Evolution for Autonomous Investing: A Multi-Agent LLM System with an Invariant Core

治理式自主投资的自我进化：具有不变核心的多智能体LLM系统

LLM agents that act on live Web data must combine heterogeneous evidence, remember what they have done and improve from outcomes without weakening their own safeguards. We study this problem in autonomous investing, where every action is irreversible and based on untrusted Web content. We present Steward, a multi-agent LLM system that draws the boundary of self-evolution in its type system. First, nine role-specialised agents decide jointly: four analysts read news feeds, SEC filings, macroeconomic series and market data and emit typed reports, from which a Portfolio Manager sets target positions that a protected Risk Manager audits. Second, a seven-layer shared memory, assembled by code from the system's own record, gives each decision its position context, the market narrative, calibration and the agent's past decisions. Third, the system improves itself by review: code grades every trade and forecast nightly and writes the grades into memory, and each quarter a reflector proposes prompt learnings that a deterministic gate applies reversibly and replay of recorded inputs verifies. All three operate around an invariant core of order arithmetic, risk rules and discipline-agent prompts that no self-edit can reach. In a five-month live deployment (102 trading days, 1,632 LLM calls), the system contained a 24.6% sector drawdown to a 0.3% loss; in replay of its recorded inputs, a single model given the same raw data traded more and lost 1.35 points against the market over five days where the joint design showed no significant loss, and no injected instruction was followed; and a 2015-2026 backtest shows the core alone lowering the maximum drawdown under all 50 random entry policies. The reflector traced the dominant failure to its root cause, and the gate applied a learning that the agent adopted on trigger evenings and on no control evening. Evolving with Open Worlds: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement for Future Forecasting

Most contemporary artificial intelligence (AI) systems are released with frozen parameters and improve through external updates, whereas an ideal system would keep improving from its own experience, a capacity we call recursive self-improvement (RSI). Yet studying RSI requires an environment that is continuously changing, objectively verifiable, and naturally grounded, posing a fundamental challenge for agent evolution. Here we introduce EvoForesight, a sustainable evolution agent system grounded in future event prediction, in which agents continuously interact with a dynamically evolving environment and improve by internalizing and externalizing the experience gained through these interactions. A living environment synthesizes verifiable tasks, and a recursive-evolution engine turns the reasoning trajectory behind each prediction back into capability: decoupling search from prediction adapts parameter-level evolution to the contamination-prone live environment, while distilling reusable skills lets the agent evolve from the limited live trajectories such an environment affords. On the external FutureX leaderboard, which evaluates predictions against real-world outcomes and is updated weekly, our self-improved forecaster ranked first across all systems over three consecutive weeks, achieving up to a 195% improvement in rating. On our synthesized evaluation, it ranked among the top three across four base LLMs, outperforming the strongest baseline by up to 10.7 Bradley--Terry points. More broadly, these results suggest that a system built for self-improvement is best understood as an agent that co-evolves with a genuinely living, open-ended environment, pointing to a shift in AI from training static models on fixed data toward cultivating agents that grow stronger from their own experience, with such an open world as the indispensable basis of that growth.

Intraday Forecasting Ability and Liquidity Provision of Institutional Traders

This study uses a high-frequency dataset from a stock exchange to examine how forecasting ability and liquidity provision relate to institutional traders' proprietary intraday profitability. We discover persistent forecasting abilities among traders. Specifically, we demonstrate that hypothetical flow-following strategies based on the execution flows of these skillful traders yield annualized Sharpe ratios ranging from 16 to 63 across various trading frequencies. We match buy and sell executions into positions, linking realized profit and loss (P&L) to horizon-matched forecasting proxies and the liquidity roles of both executions. Our findings indicate a direct correlation between improved forecasting ability and profitability, where a one-standard-deviation increase in the forecasting ability proxy is associated with 1.02 -- 1.19 currency units higher P&L per matched position. In these regressions, P&L is 1.28 -- 1.31 currency units higher for one passive execution than for none, and a further 3.79 -- 3.82 higher for two than for one, underscoring the significant financial benefits of liquidity provision.

Together, the UQ and XAI analyses provide a transparent framework for uncertainty-aware neural calibration and downstream exotic pricing under the specified prior-predictive model.

Solvable models of learning to pursue a moving target

可解的学习追求移动目标模型

Agents tasked with intercepting a moving target must learn how to reach it, ideally as efficiently as possible. As observers of animal pursuit behavior have noticed, different strategies are possible: on one extreme, the agent reactively moves toward the target's current location; on the other, the agent predicts the target's future location and moves directly there. Motivated by the desire to understand how such strategies might be learned, we introduce a continuous-time linear-quadratic model of open-loop target pursuit whose optimal control strategies interpolate between these possibilities. Usefully, a small number of interpretable parameters control which type of strategy is optimal, and it is possible to derive closed-form solutions for optimal strategies, policy learning dynamics, and value learning dynamics. Exploiting our model's linear structure, we find that the time scales of learning precisely correspond to the eigenvalues of certain matrices, and that relevant eigenvalue spectra generically have a gap. We show that this gap indicates that agents tend to learn how to reach their target before optimizing their movement. Our results provide a detailed mathematical characterization of pursuit behavior and its learning dynamics, which can both serve as a benchmark for empirical work, and as a foundation for more elaborate theoretical treatments of pursuit.

双重稳健的分布鲁棒离线上下文定价

Offline contextual pricing often relies on logged observational data, but faces challenges from distributional shifts between training and deployment environments. Distributionally robust optimization (DRO) provides a principled approach to off-policy evaluation and learning (OPE/L). However, existing methods are mostly limited to discrete actions. Recent work has explored DRO for continuous treatments using inverse propensity weighting (IPW), while such IPW-based estimators can be sensitive to the convergence rate of propensity score estimates, particularly when estimated nonparametrically, which may lead to larger estimation errors and regret. In this work, we develop a doubly robust (DR) framework for distributionally robust OPE/L in continuous pricing settings. For evaluation, we propose a localized DR estimator that addresses the computational challenges of worst-case expectations by fitting only a small number of regressions, comparable to standard non-robust DR, while achieving semiparametric efficiency under mild product rate conditions. For learning, we leverage the inherent smoothness of demand noise to handle pricing-specific discontinuities in revenue outcomes (e.g., threshold-based purchase decisions), establishing a finite-sample regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_p(T^{-s/(2s+1)})$ for smoothness orders $s=1,2$. This bound improves upon existing regret rates in existing DRO-based off-policy learning (OPL) for continuous treatments. Extensive experiments under various levels of distribution shift validate our proposed framework.

DISC: Domain-Integrated Similarity Cost for Time-Series Dataset

时间序列数据集分析的领域集成相似性成本方法

The growth of time-series datasets and foundation models has made dataset comparison a central part of time-series workflows. Assessing source-target transfer before model adaptation requires similarity estimates that can summarize transfer-related dataset relationships without exhaustive source-target evaluation over every candidate pair. Many metrics have been proposed for measuring similarity between time-series datasets, but they capture different dataset relationships and vary in how well they align with downstream utility across tasks. We evaluate this gap by measuring the correlation between source-target similarity scores and downstream forecasting errors in out-of-distribution and transfer-learning settings. We propose Domain-Integrated Similarity Cost (DISC), a learnable aggregation framework that uses calibration transfer observations to select, weight, and combine similarity metrics for different downstream tasks. By integrating complementary metric values, DISC better distinguishes source-target dataset differences that matter for downstream behavior. After calibration, DISC scores source-target pairs using only their similarity-metric values and yields higher alignment than single-metric references on the reported split. These results show that time-series dataset similarity is task-dependent, and that learned metric integration provides a stronger proxy for downstream transfer behavior than manually selecting one similarity metric.
