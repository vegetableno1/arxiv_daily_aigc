Satisficing Regret Minimization in Bandits with Knapsacks

基于背包问题的满足遗憾最小化多臂老虎机

We study satisficing regret minimization in bandits with knapsacks (BwK), where a learner sequentially selects randomized policies, i.e., distributions over arms, to obtain mean rewards above a satisficing threshold as frequently as possible while respecting finite resource budgets. The performance is measured by satisficing regret, the cumulative deficits of the selected policies' mean rewards below this threshold. Resource constraints introduce two difficulties: an algorithm can incur linear satisficing regret even with small standard regret as deficits to the threshold cannot be compensated, and random consumption can cause early resource depletion even when policies are feasible in expectation. To address these challenges, we propose SELECTwK, a general algorithmic template that uses a BwK learning oracle algorithm with sub-linear expected (standard) regret upper bound to search for and test candidate satisficing policies (i.e., policies whose mean rewards exceed satisficing threshold and whose expected resource consumption is feasible over the time horizon). Our regret analysis, supported by an LP-duality argument, establishes that SELECTwK attains constant expected satisficing regret when a satisficing policy exists. We further introduce a stronger regret condition that bounds the expected sum of positive reward deficits to the optimal one at each time step, under which SELECTwK achieves a sharper constant bound together with a sub-linear bound in the time horizon. As a complement, SELECTwK still preserves the oracle's sub-linear expected standard regret bound if the satisficing threshold is unattainable. Finally, we establish the oracle conditions and corresponding satisficing regret bound for existing finite-armed BwK and dynamic pricing algorithms, and conduct numerical experiments on dynamic pricing with limited inventory to demonstrate the empirical performance of SELECTwK.

The methodology includes LP-duality arguments and establishes constant expected satisficing regret when feasible policies exist.

Dynamic Strategy Selection

动态策略选择

We consider the problem of dynamically selecting among competing strategies using observable predictors. To mitigate estimation noise and excessive switching, we regularise the estimated policy with a switching cost, which may exceed actual transaction costs when these are too small to provide sufficient regularisation. Since switching costs make current choices affect future decisions, we formulate the problem using dynamic programming. The optimal policy is estimated using a reinforcement learning algorithm based on iterated regressions, making the procedure simple to implement. We apply the method to monthly rotation among momentum, value, profitability, and defensive U.S. equity factors over a sample of about 60 years. Using a parsimonious set of state variables, the estimated policy outperforms both the myopic dynamic policy and the best fixed factor in hindsight in out-of-sample evaluation. Under weak conditions, we show theoretically that the decision rule estimated from empirical data is asymptotically consistent for the unknown optimal decision rule. Simulation results illustrate the finite-sample relevance of these asymptotic results.

核心方法： The paper introduces a reinforcement learning-based approach for dynamically selecting among competing strategies (momentum, value, profitability, defensive factors) with observable predictors. It employs dynamic programming to account for switching costs, using iterated regressions for policy estimation.

Calibration of Passive Market Impact with a Queue-Sensitive Propagator Model

基于队列敏感传播模型的被动市场影响校准

We develop a framework for calibrating passive market impact, defined as the price response to liquidity-providing limit orders, in contrast to the impact of liquidity-consuming market orders. We build on the model of Ouazzani Chahdi, Rosenbaum, and Szymanski (arxiv:2412.07461), which combines Hawkes-driven market-order flow with queue-reactive limit-order and cancellation dynamics. Within this model, we show that the price decomposes into a universal propagator term driven by signed order-flow imbalance and a state-dependent correction that captures the dependence of price sensitivity on volume at the best quotes. This decomposition links limit-order-book microstructure to standard propagator models and yields a regression on two observable signals: the propagator signal and the queue-weighted order-flow imbalance. We calibrate the model on high-frequency order-book data and find that the passive-impact term explains substantial variation in prices beyond the classical propagator, improving both model fit and the accuracy of predicted impact curves.

Decomposable Reward Modeling and Realistic Environment Design for Reinforcement Learning-Based Forex Trading

可分解奖励建模与真实环境设计在基于强化学习的外汇交易中的应用

Reinforcement learning (RL) for foreign exchange (Forex) trading requires realistic execution modeling, interpretable reward design, and action spaces that respect market constraints. Existing approaches often simplify these aspects, limiting policy transparency and practical relevance. This paper presents a modular RL framework integrating three components: (1) a friction-aware execution engine enforcing anti-lookahead semantics---observations at close_t, execution at open_t+1, and mark-to-market at close_t+1---while modeling spread, commission, slippage, rollover financing, and margin-triggered liquidation; (2) a decomposable 11-component reward architecture with fixed, pre-specified weights and per-step logging, enabling attribution, ablation, and auditability; and (3) a 10-action discrete interface supporting scaling, reduction, closure, and reversal, with legal-action masking enforcing margin-aware feasibility during training and evaluation. Empirical evaluation on EURUSD training-split data reveals non-monotonic interactions among reward components, demonstrating that additional penalties do not consistently improve performance. The full reward configuration achieves a terminal training Sharpe ratio of 0.765 and cumulative return of 57.09%. Comparing action spaces, the extended 10-action interface increases cumulative return but reduces Sharpe relative to a conservative 3-action variant, revealing a return-activity trade-off under a fixed training budget. Scaling-enabled strategies consistently reduce drawdowns, with the combined configuration delivering the strongest endpoint performance. Supplementary diagnostics on GBPUSD, USDJPY, and AUDUSD exhibit qualitatively similar trends. These findings demonstrate the framework's diagnostic value and underscore the importance of controlled, interpretable design in RL-based financial systems.

Each agent generates an independently testable forecast through a common interface, while leakage-safe historical validation measures its predictive skill relative to a naive reference. Code, data, and results are available at https://github.com/Madumita24/FACTS-MAS.
