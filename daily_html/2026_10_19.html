We study the conditional distribution of limit-order volume submitted over the next five minutes, given the current order book and recent order flow. The task requires capturing price placement, total volume, and predictive uncertainty jointly. Coordinate-wise diffusion losses do not explicitly account for price displacement, while normalizing fields to unit mass removes information about total volume. Look Back, Correct Forward: Error Feedback Calibration for Time Series Forecasting

回顾修正前瞻：时间序列预测中的误差反馈校准

Time series forecasting models are increasingly deployed in continuously evolving environments. After a forecast is issued, future observations gradually arrive and reveal how the model has failed. Existing methods mainly treat such observations as supervision for parameter updates. However, it remains unclear whether verified forecasting errors can themselves provide causal feedback for correcting future predictions. To address this, we introduce Error Feedback Calibration (EFC), a fully test-time framework for deterministic post-hoc correction of time series forecasting. EFC constructs a Causal Error Diagonal (CED) from revealed errors of overlapping historical forecasts, providing a fresh view of the frozen forecaster's recent failure behavior. Then Cumulative Error Transport (CET) maps this error state to future residuals using cumulative estimation, while temporal out-of-sample validation controls the correction strength. Temporal Residual Learning (TRL) further predicts only the residual structure left after CET from recent CED trajectories, rather than relearning the entire forecasting error. Extensive experiments across forecasting backbones and datasets show that EFC broadly improves forecasting performance while substantially controlling harmful over correction.

核心方法： Error Feedback Calibration (EFC) framework that provides post-hoc correction for time series forecasting by analyzing historical forecast errors and mapping them to future residuals using Cumulative Error Transport (CET) and Temporal Residual Learning (TRL).
数据来源： Time series data from financial markets (e.g., stock prices, FX rates, commodity prices), could be adapted to various market data types.

Time-partitioning Gaussian Process Mixture Model

时序分割高斯过程混合模型

Nonstationarity and heterogeneous dynamics are common in financial and macroeconomic time series. Volatility alternates between calm and turbulent regimes, interest rates follow monetary-policy cycles, and major events trigger persistent structural shifts. We propose the Time-partitioning Gaussian Process Mixture Model (TPGPMM), a Bayesian nonparametric framework for regime identification and nonstationary prediction that infers such regimes and their within-regime dependence from data, without Markovian assumptions. It combines (i) an adaptive Pitman-Yor process prior on ordered compositions, mapped to block lengths with contiguity enforced by construction, and (ii) regime-specific Gaussian processes for temporal dependence, yielding a nonstationary predictive distribution. By inferring its discount parameter, the prior adapts to homogeneous and highly heterogeneous regime lengths. Unlike sequential or Markov-switching methods, it flags persistent structural shifts as change points rather than reacting to transient shocks. A split-merge Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler with exact Gibbs updates of regime boundaries jointly learns change points and hyperparameters in batch, quantifying uncertainty over the partition and within-regime dynamics. With an RBF kernel and a fixed, data-informed noise level, TPGPMM produces a sparse, confident segmentation. On simulated data it recovers the true regimes, and on the CBOE Volatility Index and the 10-year US Treasury yield it identifies regimes aligned with documented market and monetary-policy events. For prediction, variants estimating the noise level fit better than change-point baselines and fit and forecast better than the main specification, at the cost of a less sparse or less event-aligned segmentation; with a regime mean, they fit better than a fitted stationary Gaussian process and have lower forecast RMSE except at the shortest horizons.

核心方法： Bayesian nonparametric framework combining adaptive Pitman-Yor process prior with regime-specific Gaussian processes for nonstationary time-series modeling, using a split-merge Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler for joint learning of change points and hyperparameters

From Alpha to Execution: Trading Horizons, Regimes, and Schedules under Concave Impact and Hurst Scaling

从Alpha到执行：在凹性冲击与赫斯特标度下的交易期限、状态与调度

Classical execution models assume linear impact, diffusive prices, and an exogenous horizon. We study execution under concave power-law impact and Hurst-scaled price fluctuations, and make two contributions. First, we choose the execution horizon by maximizing the Sharpe ratio of the trade's realized P&L. Price moves during the execution window are part of that P&L, so executing more slowly reduces its variance, and because impact is concave it can also lower cost per share. Urgency comes only from alpha realized while the order is being worked. The problem is governed by the signs of two quantities: (i) the duration elasticity of impact $a$, which under a structural prior equals the impact-persistence gap $\beta - H$ and can take either sign; and (ii) the net urgency $\nu$, the rate of alpha realization net of exposure relief. When $\nu$ keeps its sign over the window, an interior optimum exists, to leading order in $T/t_f$, only when both are positive; otherwise the order should be worked at the fastest or slowest admissible pace. In the interior regime the first-order condition involves no impact coefficient, order size or volume, and the objective is flat: for a large-cap example, the window within 1% of maximal Sharpe spans more than an order of magnitude. Second, given the window, we derive the schedule shape by convex programming under an envelope cost. For any increasing per-share cost of delay $w(t)$, the KKT solution is $\phi \propto (\lambda - w(t))_+^{1/\beta}$. For a Hurst-scaled envelope $w \propto t^H$, the normalized schedule depends only on $(\beta, H)$, has a closed form via the incomplete beta function, is front-loaded, and tapers smoothly to zero, saving 10-17% of envelope cost relative to a constant-rate (TWAP) schedule of equal duration. Whether it uses the whole window depends on a single dimensionless robustness level, for which we give a calibration.

核心方法： The paper introduces an execution optimization framework that combines concave power-law impact models with Hurst-scaled price fluctuations. It provides two key contributions: 1) A methodology for determining optimal execution horizons by maximizing Sharpe ratios of realized P&L, incorporating both alpha decay and impact dynamics; 2) A convex programming approach for deriving optimal schedule shapes under an envelope cost constraint, with closed-form solutions via incomplete beta functions.

Algorithm Modelization: Transforming Optimization Algorithms into Evolvable Intelligence

算法模型化：将优化算法转化为可进化的智能

Can algorithms be learned and evolved as models, rather than repeatedly redesigned as programs? We explore this question by introducing Algorithm Modelization, a new paradigm that turns iterative algorithms into trainable models. Its central idea is to regard algorithm execution trajectories as computational experience: by learning how algorithmic states evolve over iterations, a model can internalize the knowledge embodied in existing algorithms, reproduce their computational behaviors, and further evolve beyond them through training. As a first realization of this paradigm, we modelize iterative optimization as an autoregressive process, where preceding generations constitute the computational context for generating the next population. We develop a dimension-independent population-temporal architecture to capture both population interactions and iterative dynamics, together with an imitation-to-surpassing learning framework that first acquires algorithmic knowledge from heterogeneous teachers and then improves the resulting optimizer directly through optimization feedback. The modelized optimizer consequently goes beyond reproducing individual algorithms: it consolidates heterogeneous search knowledge into a single trainable model, achieves stronger optimization capability through subsequent improvement, and generalizes its acquired knowledge across problems and substantially larger unseen dimensions. More fundamentally, Algorithm Modelization makes algorithmic capability accumulable through learning, where new algorithms and problem-solving experience can become training resources for progressively improving the model. Our results establish a promising path from manually designed algorithms toward general algorithm models that learn, generalize, and evolve from computational experience.
